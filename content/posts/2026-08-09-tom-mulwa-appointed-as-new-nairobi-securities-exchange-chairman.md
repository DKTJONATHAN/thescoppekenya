## Capital Markets Oversight or Corporate Pipeline? Tom Mulwa Appointed as New Nairobi Securities Exchange Chairman is the central subject of the update.

The Nairobi Securities Exchange has appointed Tom Mulwa as its new chairman, tasked with a lofty ambition to swell retail investor ranks to nine million. While the exchange celebrates this transition as a bold vision for domestic wealth, the math behind these numbers remains anchored to the same stagnation that has haunted the bourse for decades.

### The Math of Nine Million

The target of nine million retail investors is not merely ambitious; it is statistically detached from the current reality of the Nairobi Securities Exchange. Historical growth rates over the last decade show a market characterized by apathy, where the barrier to entry remains high and investor confidence is regularly shaken by poor corporate governance and thin liquidity. Achieving such a massive influx of participants requires more than just technological platitudes or promises of modernization. It requires a fundamental shift in how the exchange interacts with the working class.

If this figure is to be taken as a serious policy goal, one must look at how the NSE intends to convert a population currently obsessed with [live football betting](https://zandani.co.ke/article/live-football-betting-in-kenya-fast-action-secretbet-mobile) into long-term capital market participants. The exchange has repeatedly failed to provide the necessary incentives for the average Kenyan to view the stock market as anything other than a casino for the elite. Without a radical restructuring of fees and a crackdown on market manipulation, this nine million target functions more as a PR smokescreen than a genuine roadmap for financial inclusion.

### Conflicts at the Summit

The real story of this appointment lies in the architecture of influence surrounding Mulwa. As he takes the helm of the exchange, he continues to maintain a significant footprint at Liaison Group, a regional financial services firm with deep interests in the very markets the NSE hopes to capture. This creates a vertical integration of influence that should alarm those who value an impartial, transparent bourse. When the chairman of the exchange oversees the regulatory and strategic direction of the market while holding leadership positions in private financial entities, the line between institutional growth and private gain blurs.

This situation is compounded by Mulwa’s presence on the National Investment Council, a body established to advise on economic policy. By sitting on both the council and the board of the NSE, he creates a direct loop between government policy and market regulation. This is not the first time the exchange has seen such cozy relationships between its leadership and the broader investment sector, but the scale of influence here is unprecedented. The risk is that the retail expansion mandate will be used to steer traffic and capital toward specific affiliated institutions under the guise of democratizing the market.

Just as the public scrutinizes the [financial growth of Safaricom under Peter Ndegwa](https://zandani.co.ke/article/peter-ndegwa-safaricom-ceo-tenure-financial-growth), the movement of capital under the NSE board requires similar vigilance. The exchange has spent years talking about expansion while the actual number of active, liquid retail accounts remains dismal. If the strategy is simply to use the NSE as a funnel for the products and services managed by the chairman's private interests, the average Kenyan will remain a spectator to the process rather than a participant in the wealth.

Is this appointment designed to finally open the gates for the common investor, or has the NSE simply handed the keys to the gatekeeper who already owns the toll booth?
