Homa Bay Governor Gladys Wanga and Member of Parliament Peter Kaluma are facing intense political backlash this week as they attempt to block the Linda Mwananchi activist group from conducting oversight activities regarding the deteriorating state of local road infrastructure in Homa Bay. ## The Homa Bay Power Struggle and Road Infrastructure

The conflict escalated after Governor Wanga and MP Kaluma moved to restrict activist Okiya Omtatah and his associates from organizing public meetings within the county. While the local administration claims these efforts are necessary to maintain political order, critics argue the actions are a calculated attempt to suppress mounting public anger over impassable roads and neglected infrastructure projects that have crippled local travel and commerce.

### The Blame Game Tactics
A persistent strategy used by Homa Bay leadership involves shifting the responsibility for regional infrastructure failures onto the national government. By framing the central government as a convenient scapegoat for the lack of progress, local officials seek to deflect attention from their own budget management and development priorities. This diversionary tactic has become increasingly transparent to residents, who note that the constitutional mandate to maintain county-level transport networks rests firmly with the devolved government. The recurring assertion that national interference is the primary cause of local decay is now viewed by many as an exhausted excuse to avoid accountability for the current state of the region.

### Accountability versus Noise
The Linda Mwananchi group contends that the hostility directed toward them is a direct response to the scrutiny they have applied to county spending. Activists argue that if the road network were properly maintained, there would be no need for external oversight. Public sentiment suggests that the energy spent by leaders on silencing dissent would be far more productive if redirected toward infrastructure repair. Residents are increasingly rejecting the narrative that local leaders are powerless against national authorities, pointing out that other jurisdictions have successfully managed their own road improvements despite similar bureaucratic challenges.

### The Reality of Devolved Leadership
The current political tension highlights a growing divide between elected officials and the public they represent. Voters in Homa Bay are demanding a shift away from political posturing toward measurable results in infrastructure development. As the rainy season approaches and road conditions worsen, the administration faces increasing pressure to prioritize maintenance over legal and political obstruction. The consensus among the local population remains clear: they are no longer satisfied with defensive rhetoric and are instead focused on the immediate necessity of safe, navigable roads that support the daily economic life of the county.
