## The Phantom Practitioners of Kenyan Pharmacy Aden Duale clarifies new pharmacy standards for diploma holders is the central subject of the update.

Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale claims the new pharmacy standards protect patients, yet the professional uproar masks a multi-billion shilling shadow economy where licenses are rented out like cheap real estate while qualified personnel remain sidelined.

### The Shadow Economy of License Renting

The core of the current friction is not merely a debate over academic credentials or job titles. It is a calculated strike at the lucrative practice of license renting, a systemic blight that has turned pharmacies into fronts for absentee owners. In this grey market, degree-holding superintendents lease their credentials to facility owners for a monthly fee, never stepping foot inside the premises. This arrangement allows profit-driven investors to run complex health facilities without maintaining the professional oversight required by law.

By tightening the strings on who can manage specific facility categories, the Ministry of Health is effectively attempting to burst this bubble. When a pharmacy is allowed to operate under the signature of a person who is not present, the patient becomes the primary victim. Drugs are dispensed by unqualified staff, inventories remain unchecked, and the critical link between medicine and expert advice is severed. The government claims these 2026 standards aim to formalize the industry, but for the owners who have built their business models on rented credentials, this regulation is an existential threat to their margins.

### Profit Margins Over Patient Safety

The economic disparity between diploma-holding technologists and degree-holding superintendents creates a distorted labor market. Degree holders carry the legal burden of a facility, often charging a premium to "rent" their names to pharmacies they do not supervise. This creates a scenario where the person physically behind the counter-the diploma holder-is often underpaid and overworked, while the "owner" collects rent on a license that exists in name only.

As seen in other sectors, such as the [systemic market barriers hindering youth agribusiness in Kenya](https://zandani.co.ke/article/systemic-market-barriers-hindering-youth-agribusiness-kenya), the struggle for professional legitimacy is hampered by gatekeepers who profit from the status quo. The Ministry suggests that Level 3 facilities require a higher tier of oversight to ensure standard operating procedures are followed. However, this creates a bottleneck. If diploma holders are legally restricted from serving as superintendents in these facilities, the current stock of degree holders will likely increase their rental rates, further inflating the cost of running a compliant pharmacy. It is a cycle that keeps the supply of labor artificial and the cost of healthcare for the average citizen unnecessarily high.

### The Myth of Professional Inclusion

Duale maintains that five years of consultation went into these standards, yet the Kenya Pharmaceutical Association remains unconvinced. The disconnect lies in the lack of a clear career progression framework for diploma holders. The industry is currently divided between those who view pharmacies as high-margin retail businesses and those who view them as clinical sites.

While the ministry talks about safety, the real conversation revolves around who controls the distribution of essential medicines. If the state forces a professional audit of every facility, many of these illicit operations will fold. The resistance to the 2026 standards is not just a fight for job descriptions; it is a fight to protect an ecosystem where credentials are bought and sold rather than earned through practice. Whether this crackdown on the shadow economy will lead to actual improvements in patient safety or simply push the rental market deeper underground remains the primary concern.

If the goal is truly to sanitize the sector and dismantle the rental culture, why does the government not pursue the owners of these facilities with the same intensity as they regulate the diploma-holding staff who work the shifts?
