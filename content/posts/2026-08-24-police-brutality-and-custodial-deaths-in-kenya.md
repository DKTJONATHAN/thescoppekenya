## The State-Sanctioned Myth of Custodial Suicide Systemic Police Brutality and Custodial Deaths Rise in Kenya is the central subject of the update.

Whenever a detainee dies in police custody, the official narrative arrives with suspicious haste. Before the body has even cooled, authorities are almost certain to declare the death a suicide. This reflexive dismissal has become the default mechanism of institutional self-preservation, a convenient fiction that relies on the public’s exhaustion and the families' grief to obscure mounting evidence of state-sponsored violence.

The "suicidal suspect" trope is a sophisticated form of bureaucratic gaslighting. Consider the case of 19-year-old Eric Otieno, who died while held at Muthaiga. The state’s explanation suggests he simply decided to end his life in a holding cell. It is a cold, calculated bet: by labeling these deaths as self-inflicted, the police effectively insulate themselves from scrutiny. It voids the need for rigorous internal inquiries and protects the department’s reputation. Yet, this story is increasingly falling apart as forensic reality contradicts the official record.

### Decoding the Pathology of Violence

A growing chorus of independent pathologists is systematically dismantling the state’s suicide defense. These medical professionals have documented clear physical indicators of trauma, including strangulation marks, internal hemorrhaging, and blunt force injuries that are biologically impossible to inflict through hanging. When looking at the autopsy of Albert Ojwang, whose death led to the arrest of six officers, the evidence did not just suggest foul play; it demanded it. 

These experts are not lobbyists; they are technicians presenting biological facts. When a pathologist identifies clear signs of torture, the police claim of self-harm ceases to be a theory and becomes a transparent insult to the public.

This is not a string of accidental anomalies. The Independent Policing Oversight Authority recently recorded 20 custodial deaths in just four months, providing a grim, statistical profile of systemic rot. When authorities consistently record these as suicides, they are not investigating; they are participating in a cover-up. The sheer statistical frequency of these deaths reveals that police are not simply failing in their duty of care. They are actively utilizing the suicide label as a shield against the legal consequences of their own brutality. Whether the victim is Erick Kiptanui Koros or an anonymous individual buried by the system, the script remains identical. The police dictate the cause of death, and the state provides the silence.

### The Psychological Toll of Institutional Deception

Beyond the initial violence, the state inflicts a secondary, deeper trauma on the families of the deceased. Losing a loved one is agony, but having that death reframed as an act of personal despair by state agents is a distinct form of cruelty. Families are forced into a labyrinthine legal battle where they must disprove the state’s baseless narratives while the very institution in control of the evidence obstructs them at every turn. 

This is a strategy of attrition. By blaming the deceased, the state shifts the conversation from police misconduct to the supposed mental instability of victims who are no longer alive to defend themselves.

We must stop treating these tragedies as isolated administrative blunders. Instead, we should view them as a deliberate, coordinated effort to maintain total impunity. The state fears the precedent of successful prosecutions, such as the ongoing legal actions concerning the death of Albert Ojwang. As long as the suicide designation remains the automatic default for every death in custody, reform will remain a hollow fantasy. Accountability is not achieved by asking how these people died, but by interrogating the systemic fabrication of the initial reports. The lie itself is a crime. Until we hold those who sign off on these falsified narratives responsible, the cycle of state violence will continue without interruption.
