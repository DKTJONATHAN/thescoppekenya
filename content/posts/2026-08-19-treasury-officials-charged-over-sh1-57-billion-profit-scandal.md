## How 1.57 Billion Shillings Vanished from Our Food Security Basket Treasury officials charged over Sh1.57 billion PROFIT scandal is the central subject of the update.

Nine individuals, including high-ranking Treasury officials, appeared before Magistrate Rose Ndobi at the Milimani Anti-Corruption Court yesterday to answer for the alleged theft of 1.57 billion shillings intended to bolster agricultural productivity for small-scale farmers across the country.

### What was the PROFIT programme supposed to deliver?

The Programme for Rural Outreach of Financial Innovations and Technologies, known as PROFIT, was designed as a lifeline for rural agricultural communities. Its core mandate involved providing subsidized credit, improving access to farm inputs like fertilizers, and building the necessary market infrastructure to help smallholder farmers move beyond subsistence. By easing the financial burden of production, the initiative aimed to stabilize food supplies and lower the cost of basic commodities for the average Kenyan household. Instead of reaching the intended fields, investigators allege that these funds were siphoned off through complex financial maneuvers, leaving the government's promise of agricultural transformation on empty shelves.

### What could these missing billions have achieved for farmers?

When 1.57 billion shillings disappears from the public purse, the impact is felt far beyond the courtroom. For context, this amount could have fully subsidized the fertilizer requirements for thousands of households across the grain belt for multiple planting seasons. Alternatively, it could have funded the construction of climate-resilient storage facilities and local irrigation projects that are currently lacking in many rural regions. The absence of this capital translates directly into lower yields and higher market prices for basic staples. While large-scale agricultural players might utilize cold chain logistics to secure their margins, as seen in sectors like avocado exports, the small-scale farmer remains vulnerable. Without the intended financial support from the PROFIT programme, these farmers have been left to face rising input costs and market volatility alone, directly contributing to the current inflationary pressure on the food basket.

### How did the court proceedings unfold?

During the session at the Milimani Anti-Corruption Court, the prosecution leveled 121 counts against the nine suspects, ranging from abuse of office to money laundering. The proceedings underscored the scale of the alleged financial misconduct, which spanned several years of administrative operations within the Treasury. The magistrate granted varied bail and bond terms, with some individuals facing requirements as high as 20 million shillings, while others were granted terms starting at 300,000 shillings. The accused are also required to surrender their passports as a condition of their release, ensuring they remain available for the trial which is scheduled to reconvene on September 2.

The gravity of this case reaches into every kitchen in Kenya. Every shilling diverted from agricultural development is a shilling that makes a bag of maize or a kilo of vegetables more expensive for a family struggling to get by. While the legal process will now take its course to determine the culpability of those charged, the systemic loss remains a heavy burden on the agricultural sector. True food security relies not just on ideal weather conditions, but on the integrity of the institutions that manage the resources meant to keep our farmers productive and our food affordable.

Corruption in agricultural funding turns the dream of national food self-sufficiency into a high-cost reality for every citizen.
