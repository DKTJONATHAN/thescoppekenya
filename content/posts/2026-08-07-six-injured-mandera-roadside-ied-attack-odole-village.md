## Blood on the Mandera Road: Another Failure of Intelligence Six injured in Mandera roadside IED attack near Odole Village is the central subject of the update.

Six residents of Odole Village are currently recovering at the Elwak Sub-County Referral Hospital after their vehicle triggered an improvised explosive device on Thursday evening. While officials categorize this as a militant-led security incident, it is the latest evidence of a systemic collapse in border protection protocols.

### The Illusion of Road-Sweeping Protocols

The recurring nature of these explosions in Mandera County suggests that the Ministry of Interior’s promised security enhancements are little more than ink on paper. Residents of the border region continue to report an eerie lack of proactive road-sweeping measures despite constant warnings of extremist infiltration. This incident is not an anomaly. It is the predictable outcome of a security strategy that prioritizes reactive patrols over consistent, intelligence-led route clearance.

Between the high-level policy meetings in Nairobi and the reality of the dusty, dangerous roads near the Somalia border, there exists a fatal vacuum of accountability. Public budgets for counter-terrorism and regional surveillance remain substantial, yet the basic task of ensuring that civilian transport routes are free of lethal obstacles remains unfulfilled. Locals recount a familiar pattern: promises of increased military presence and advanced mine-detection equipment are made immediately following a blast, only to evaporate as soon as the media cycle moves on to lighter topics like the [Treasury Bill Rates Rise as Central Bank Pauses Rate Cuts](https://zandani.co.ke/article/treasury-bill-rates-rise-central-bank-pauses-cuts). Meanwhile, civilians continue to traverse routes that are essentially minefields waiting for a trigger.

### Counting Costs While Citizens Pay the Price

Security agencies often point to the vast, porous nature of the border to justify why militant groups are able to plant devices with impunity. However, this excuse ignores the specific geography of these attacks, which frequently occur along known transit corridors. When the state claims to be winning the war on terror, the residents of Mandera find those words hollow. The deployment of resources seems to be concentrated on urban hubs rather than the periphery where the threat is most acute and persistent.

While the country focuses its collective attention elsewhere-from debates over [Nicholas Kioko Pushes for Public Holiday for Domestic Workers](https://zandani.co.ke/article/nicholas-kioko-proposes-public-holiday-for-domestic-workers) to the frenzy of the [Aston Villa face Nottingham Forest in Europa League semi-final](https://zandani.co.ke/article/aston-villa-face-nottingham-forest-in-europa-league-semi-final)-the families of the six injured victims are left to navigate the financial and physical trauma of their recovery. Investigations have been launched, but for the people of Odole Village, the label of "investigation" has become synonymous with delay rather than resolution. If the intelligence-led policing model was working, these devices would be discovered during transit sweeps, not by the impact of civilian vehicles.

The failure to secure these roads is not just a tactical oversight; it is an abandonment of the basic mandate to protect taxpayers. Money is allocated for border security every fiscal year, yet the tactical reality remains unchanged. The victims of this week’s blast are statistics in a government report that will likely be shelved by Monday. As the dust settles in Elwak, the silence from the relevant authorities regarding why their own protocols failed to detect this device is deafening. If the state cannot secure a single known route for its citizens, what is the actual purpose of the bloated security budget currently assigned to the northern frontier?
