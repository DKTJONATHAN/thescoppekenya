## The Governor Who Dodges the Mace

Isiolo residents are exhausted by the ongoing charade between the county headquarters and the national oversight body, as yet another arrest warrant for Governor Abdi Guyo signals a breakdown in trust between local leadership and constitutional accountability mechanisms.

The Senate Public Accounts Committee, led by Homa Bay Senator Moses Kajwang, has finally run out of patience. Following the Governor's failure to appear for a scheduled session today, the committee directed Inspector General of Police Douglas Kanja to track down and present the county boss within 10 days. This follows a frustrating cycle of missed summons that stretches back to July 9 and July 16, where last-minute excuses replaced the transparency expected from a devolved government. The pattern of missing sessions only to later challenge the committee's authority has become a defining feature of Guyo's term, leaving the oversight body looking toothless while Isiolo faces mounting questions regarding public funds and service delivery.

### Voices from the Isiolo Streets

Public sentiment in Isiolo reflects a community caught in the crossfire of this power struggle. Local business owner John Mwangi, who operates near the county offices, notes that citizens are tired of the noise. He points out that while the Governor plays hide-and-seek with Senators in Nairobi, water shortages and crumbling roads remain the reality on the ground. For him, the arrest warrant is just theatre, and he doubts the police will actually act because they have seen similar threats fizzle out before.

Civil society activist Amina Farah echoes this cynicism. She argues that the Governor has mastered the art of exploiting procedural gaps to evade scrutiny. Her concern is that every missed summons chips away at the authority of the Senate, making it easier for other leaders to ignore their constitutional duties. To her, this is not just about one man missing a meeting; it is about the erosion of the idea that a county leader is answerable to anyone.

On the other hand, some supporters of the county administration view the Senate's move as political overreach. A local community leader who requested anonymity suggested that the constant summons are designed to cripple the county government's focus. He believes the Governor is being unfairly targeted and that these legal threats are just a distraction from the real work of development. However, even he admits that the optics of the situation are poor, and the constant friction makes the county government look like it has something to hide, regardless of the merits of the specific audit queries.

### The Cycle of Impunity

This is not the first time the Senate has attempted to force a Governor's hand through the Inspector General. History shows that previous attempts to bring defiant county executives to book often stalled once legal teams entered the fray. The 10-day ultimatum is the latest, and perhaps loudest, attempt by the committee to reassert its relevance. Yet, the challenge remains that an arrest warrant is only as effective as the willingness of the security apparatus to enforce it against a sitting official.

While the capital remains fixated on these high-level clashes, most citizens are focused on their daily survival. Whether the headlines focus on [Manchester United intensifying their pursuit of Matheus Fernandes](https://zandani.co.ke/article/manchester-united-intensify-pursuit-of-matheus-fernandes) or the latest political standoff, the average Kenyan is looking for results. Even as some youths find new paths through [AI-proof careers and marketable skills](https://zandani.co.ke/article/c-in-kcse-no-stress-unlocking-marketable-skills-ai-proof-careers-and-y), there is a growing resentment that those in power seem to play by a different set of rules than the rest of the country.

The next ten days will determine whether the Senate still commands real influence or if the Isiolo Governor has effectively proven that he is untouchable. If the deadline passes without an arrest, the institution of the Senate risks being relegated to a toothless entity in the eyes of the public. The current mood in the country is one of weary anticipation, waiting to see if this is finally the day the law applies to everyone equally or just another empty threat in a long series of political grandstanding.
