## The High Cost of Market Stagnation in Homa Bay James Orengo Claims Plot to Sabotage Homa Bay Linda Mwananchi Ral is the central subject of the update.

Political tension in Homa Bay is forcing small-scale traders to choose between their daily livelihoods and safety as the August 16 Linda Mwananchi rally approaches. When rhetoric creates a climate of fear, the local economy suffers a silent, painful contraction.

Siaya Governor James Orengo has leveled accusations that external political interests are orchestrating a campaign of intimidation to clear the town of vendors and business owners ahead of the scheduled assembly. These claims involve reports that weapons have been distributed to discourage activity in the market hub, alongside logistical maneuvers at Kabunde Airstrip meant to restrict air access to the county. While political leaders argue over optics and influence, the primary victims remain the informal sector workers who rely on steady foot traffic to clear their stock.

### How does political disruption impact local market revenue?

Markets operate on the certainty of movement and access. When alerts circulate about potential unrest or organized blockades, the immediate result is a drop in consumer confidence. For a trader in Homa Bay, a day of closure or low customer turnout is not just a nuisance; it is a direct hit to household income. The informal sector thrives on the daily circulation of goods, and any policy failure that allows public spaces to become sites of intimidation creates an environment where risk outweighs potential profit.

Security in market areas should be a foundational policy of urban governance, regardless of the political climate. When security policies are sidelined by political posturing, the accountability gap grows. When the focus shifts away from service delivery, the infrastructure of the local economy is the first casualty.

### Why do intimidation tactics threaten small-scale stability?

The survival of micro-enterprises relies on the perception that the marketplace is neutral territory. Orengo alleges that strategic meetings in Naivasha set the stage for a coordinated effort to disrupt local commerce. Whether or not these allegations result in physical confrontation, the psychological effect is already in motion. Traders are being pressured to vacate their stalls, effectively pausing the engine of the local economy.

This environment makes it impossible for entrepreneurs to plan for the future or manage their supply chains effectively. If political actors treat the town square as a battlefield for their own grievances, they ignore the reality that the residents of Homa Bay are trying to participate in the national economy. True development requires an environment where business can occur without the shadow of sabotage.
