Nairobi City County emergency teams successfully contained two distinct fire outbreaks on Saturday morning, testing the operational efficiency of local disaster management crews as they balanced resources between a residential property and a congested commercial hub.

The first distress call originated from the private residence of former Nairobi Governor Mike Sonko in the early hours, prompting an immediate deployment of firefighting units. Following the stabilization of that site, teams were redirected to Eastleigh, where a second blaze engulfed the Ali Glacier hardware store located along 3rd Street. The management of these simultaneous incidents provided a baseline assessment of the current disaster response capacity within the capital.

### Operational Response and Resource Allocation

The county emergency response unit prioritized the residential scene first, ensuring containment before shifting heavy machinery to the commercial district. By the time crews arrived at the hardware store, the fire had begun to spread through the dense infrastructure typical of the Eastleigh area. Firefighters utilized specialized pressure hoses to prevent the flames from jumping to adjacent stalls, a common risk in the high-density shopping zone.

Geoffrey Mosiria, the head of Nairobi City County emergency services, oversaw the transition of equipment between the two sites. According to Mosiria, the department utilized a split-deployment strategy that kept fire engines stationed strategically across the city. This approach allowed the team to mitigate the downtime usually associated with relocating equipment between disparate zones. He noted that the logistical coordination between the two incidents was handled by a centralized command center to ensure that neither site lacked necessary water supply or personnel.
