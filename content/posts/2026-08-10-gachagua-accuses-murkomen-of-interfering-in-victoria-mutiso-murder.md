## The Unfinished Investigation at Kikuyu Police Station

The legacy of Dr. Victoria Mutiso merits a transparent pursuit of justice, yet the halls of the Kikuyu Police Station seem clouded by external influence. While public discourse is currently preoccupied with the political friction between Rigathi Gachagua and Kipchumba Murkomen, a more pressing concern persists. The integrity of the investigative process has been compromised, leaving the pursuit of truth stalled and the prospect of accountability uncertain.

### A Case of Diverted Focus

Following the tragedy on July 29, when a targeted killing occurred near Thogoto, the community expected a swift and thorough inquiry. Local authorities initially appeared proactive, taking two individuals into custody who were found near the scene. However, the momentum of the investigation changed abruptly. Reports surfaced suggesting that these suspects were released following intervention from senior figures within the Ministry of Interior.

On August 10, Rigathi Gachagua raised significant concerns regarding the efficacy of the current criminal justice process. He argued that the Directorate of Criminal Investigations and the National Intelligence Service possess specific intelligence linking identifiable actors to the murder. Instead of investigating these leads, the process appears to have shifted toward a more simplified account. Local law enforcement is reportedly under pressure to attribute the crime to a motorcycle operator and a motorist. By focusing on these individuals, the state risks creating a narrative that protects the primary organizers of the act, effectively distancing them from legal consequences.

### Institutional Pressure and Political Interference

The developments at the station highlight a recurring vulnerability in public safety institutions, where established protocols can be sidelined by directives from government officials. When the independence of investigative bodies is compromised by political loyalties, the evidence required to build a sound case often disappears. Gachagua claims that intelligence data held by state agencies is being suppressed, hindering the identification of those responsible for the orchestration of the crime.

This pattern suggests that for many citizens, the legal system can feel like an instrument controlled by those in positions of power rather than an impartial protector of rights. Much like other families currently seeking answers in unresolved disappearance cases, the relatives of Dr. Mutiso are left without closure. When police operations are susceptible to external control, the ability of the average person to find recourse through legal channels diminishes significantly.

### Demanding Forensic Accountability

While the ongoing debate between high-ranking political figures captures media attention, the deeper issue is the fragility of investigative institutions. If police departments can be directed to release murder suspects via telephone instructions, the constitutional mandate for an impartial law enforcement agency is undermined. There is now a growing call for the disclosure of findings held by intelligence agencies, which could potentially clarify the network of individuals involved in these targeted operations.

As the legal process continues, the focus must shift from the current suspects, who appear to be the subjects of a convenient narrative, to the individuals who actually carried out or ordered the crime. The question of whether there will be a formal investigation into ministerial interference at the Kikuyu station remains a critical test for the national legal framework. At present, the files concerning Dr. Victoria Mutiso serve as a stark illustration of an investigative system grappling with the impact of political pressure. As the public watches, the upcoming court mention represents a vital opportunity to determine whether the truth will finally be brought to light or if it will remain obscured by those who seek to avoid scrutiny. The resolution of this case will stand as a benchmark for the credibility of the entire criminal justice system.
