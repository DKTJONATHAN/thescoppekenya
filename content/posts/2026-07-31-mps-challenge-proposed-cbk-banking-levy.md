## Parliamentary Pushback Against Banking Levy

The National Assembly Committee on Delegated Legislation has formally challenged the Central Bank of Kenya over a proposed 0.15 per cent gross revenue levy, arguing the move risks shifting operational costs directly onto Kenyan banking consumers.

The Committee on Delegated Legislation, led by lawmakers Robert Mbui and Robert Githinji, questioned the legal foundation of the Banking Fees Regulations, 2026, during a tense session with CBK Governor Kamau Thugge. Legislators expressed concern that the proposed shift from a flat, branch-based fee structure to a percentage-based revenue model circumvents the spirit of the Banking Act as originally framed in 1994. Under the current proposal, banks would be taxed on 0.15 per cent of their total annual earnings to fund regulatory upgrades, a cost lawmakers fear will be passed down to customers through increased transaction charges on mobile money platforms and bank transfers.

Governor Thugge defended the proposal, characterizing it as a necessary evolution to ensure the regulator has the financial resources required to oversee a rapidly digitizing financial sector. He maintained that the existing fee structure, which has remained largely stagnant since the mid-nineties, no longer aligns with the operational complexities of modern monetary oversight. According to the regulator, the move would stabilize the funding of institutional supervision, ensuring that the CBK can respond to systemic risks with greater agility and technical precision.

### Dissecting the Legal Overreach

Lawmakers directed sharp scrutiny toward the penalty structures outlined in the 2026 regulations. During the inquiry, committee members questioned the transparency of these enforcement measures, noting that the regulations provide the regulator with unprecedented reach in determining financial penalties. The committee highlighted that the 1994 legislative intent of the Banking Act prioritized consumer protection and institutional stability rather than establishing the CBK as a revenue-generating body. By moving to a percentage-based levy, the committee argued, the regulator is essentially imposing an indirect tax that falls outside the mandate granted to it by the National Assembly.

Questions regarding the trickle-down impact dominated the discourse. If banks are required to contribute 0.15 per cent of their gross revenue to the regulator, financial institutions may look to recover those funds by recalibrating their pricing models. This could manifest as higher fees for M-Pesa services, increased costs for interbank transfers, and steeper charges for basic account maintenance. The committee urged the Governor to provide a detailed impact assessment demonstrating exactly how these costs will be absorbed by institutions without harming the average account holder, particularly in an economic climate already sensitive to rising transaction expenses.

### Stakeholder Consultations and Next Steps

The committee remains unconvinced that the current drafting of the regulations provides sufficient protections for the public. Members emphasized that the power to levy funds should remain within the purview of the legislature, which acts as the primary authority on taxation and fiscal policy. There was vocal skepticism regarding the lack of clear safeguards against banks opting to pass the entirety of the 0.15 per cent burden onto their client base. The CBK Governor was cautioned that any regulation which unilaterally alters the financial landscape must undergo rigorous scrutiny to ensure it does not stifle financial inclusion or punish lower-income users.

In response to the standoff, the committee declared that it would initiate a formal period of stakeholder consultation before drafting a final report for Parliament. This process is expected to include representatives from the Kenya Bankers Association, consumer advocacy groups, and financial technology stakeholders. The move delays the immediate implementation of the levy, granting affected parties an opportunity to challenge the mechanics of the 0.15 per cent calculation.

The National Assembly is expected to demand a revised version of the regulations that separates regulatory funding from commercial fee structures. Observers will be watching for the committee's final report to see if the CBK is forced to scale back its revenue targets or if the legislature proposes a cap on how much of the levy can be recovered from retail banking customers.
