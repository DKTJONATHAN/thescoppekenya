Take Damson Idris, who, despite his critically acclaimed performance as Franklin Saint on *Snowfall*, was approached by a fan convinced he actually sold crack cocaine. It's a stark example of how deeply viewers connect with characters, sometimes losing sight of the person behind the role. Similarly, Jodie Comer has recounted disturbing requests, like a fan asking her to strangle them - a chilling echo of her *Killing Eve* character, Villanelle. Aubrey Plaza also frequently faces requests from fans asking her to punch or slap them in bars. These uncomfortable situations reveal a critical gap in understanding appropriate celebrity fan encounters.

This issue isn't confined to grand public events. Ashley Benson was once questioned about her show's plot while naked in a Korean spa shower. Zooey Deschanel received unsolicited weight advice, being told she looked "50 pounds heavier on TV." Such personal intrusions, often occurring in unexpected intimate settings, emphasize the lack of formal training or standard operating procedures for celebrities, their management teams, and event organizers in handling these unique and uncomfortable situations.
