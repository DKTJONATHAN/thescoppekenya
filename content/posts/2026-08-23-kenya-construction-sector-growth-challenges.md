## The Construction Industry Stagnation

The Kenyan construction sector has achieved a 6.8 percent growth surge in 2025, yet this recovery is currently being throttled by outdated bureaucratic hurdles. It is time to address the architects of this inefficiency who prioritize gatekeeping over national economic progress.

To the developers, regulators, and government officials steering this sector: your reliance on obsolete workflows is failing the country. While the industry clawed back from a dismal 0.7 percent contraction in 2024, that 6.8 percent growth is being systematically hollowed out by your refusal to modernize. We are witnessing a clear divide between the potential of our urban centers and the reality of our stagnant administrative processes.

### The Cost of Regulatory Inertia

The financial burden on developers has reached a critical threshold, evidenced by the Construction Input Price Index climbing to 119.75 in the second quarter of 2025, up from 119.05 in the first quarter. When the cost of steel, cement, and labor continues to rise, the last thing the market needs is a regulatory system designed to trap capital in endless approval loops. By maintaining manual, paper-heavy systems in 39 of our 47 counties, you are effectively imposing an invisible tax on every project.

This is not a mere technical delay or a struggle with digital transformation. It is an active choice. When only eight counties utilize online development permitting systems, you are signaling that transparency and speed are optional. This creates an environment where rent-seeking behavior flourishes and honest developers find themselves stalled for months, waiting for signatures that should be automated. Like a boxing match where a fighter suffers a low blow-similar to the controversy seen when [Mbavu Claims Low Blow Cost Him Boxing Match Against Majembe](https://zandani.co.ke/article/mbavu-claims-low-blow-cost-him-boxing-match-against-majembe)-your refusal to modernize is a dirty tactic that hits the entire national economy where it hurts most.

### Ignoring the Professional Mandate

The Institution of Engineers of Kenya presented a roadmap for reform in May 2025, specifically targeting the crippling bottlenecks in Nairobi development approvals. They offered a path to efficiency, yet their expertise remains largely sidelined by officials who prefer the leverage provided by a discretionary, manual process. If government youth programs can be defended as vital for national development, as argued in [Denis Itumbi Defends Government Youth Programs Despite Public Criticism Over Beneficiary Ages](https://zandani.co.ke/article/denis-itumbi-defends-government-youth-programs-despite-public-critic), then surely the professional standards of our engineers deserve the same level of administrative urgency and implementation.

The failure to adopt these reforms is a direct affront to the professionals who keep our infrastructure standing. We are operating in a 2026 reality with 1990s tools. Every day that passes without a full, nationwide shift to digital permitting is a day where developers lose money and the housing deficit widens. You cannot claim to support economic growth while simultaneously strangling the very sector that builds it.

### Demanding Accountability for Growth

Innovation in construction is no longer a luxury or a competitive edge; it is the only way to keep our cities livable and our businesses solvent. The status quo, which feeds off of bureaucracy and manual oversight, has run its course. The systemic inefficiency is not accidental. It is a structure designed to benefit those who profit from delay at the expense of national development.

I am calling on the Ministry of Lands and the leadership of the 39 lagging counties to stop obstructing progress. Adopt the digital permitting systems requested by the industry experts immediately. The 6.8 percent growth we have seen is just a fraction of what is possible if you simply move the barriers out of the way. Stop hiding behind technical jargon and commit to a transparent, automated approval process before the sector retracts again. The blueprint for a functional construction industry is already on your desks; now you must choose whether to build or to bury it.
