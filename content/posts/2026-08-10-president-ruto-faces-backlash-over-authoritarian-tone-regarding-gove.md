President William Ruto is facing mounting public criticism across Kenya this month for his frequent use of absolute language when discussing government projects. His insistence that specific policy objectives must succeed regardless of opposition has prompted widespread accusations of undemocratic governance and high-handedness.

## The Rhetorical Shift in Executive Communication

### The Language of Compulsion
The President has consistently employed rigid terminology to describe his development agenda, framing government projects as inevitable mandates rather than proposals for public consideration. While administration officials argue that this language conveys strong leadership and a commitment to results, observers interpret the phrasing as a dismissal of democratic debate. By presenting his policies as non-negotiable, the President effectively signals that public feedback is unwelcome. This rhetoric strips the electorate of its agency and suggests that the administration prioritizes unilateral execution over the consensus-building required in a healthy democracy.

### The Perception of Arrogance
Public frustration is rooted in the perceived arrogance of the executive branch. Critics argue that declaring an outcome mandatory ignores the fundamental role of citizens in a democratic system. When leadership bypasses public input in favor of forceful mandates, it widens the gap between the state and the taxpayer. This approach risks alienating the very people the projects are intended to serve. Historical and contemporary precedents suggest that when authorities demand compliance without inviting participation, they inadvertently invite deeper public resentment and institutional instability.

### Aligning Policy with Public Will
The current tension highlights a growing demand for a more consultative approach to governance as the administration prepares for its upcoming political agenda. Much like failing organizations that refuse to adapt to the needs of their stakeholders, a government that operates under the assumption that citizens will simply fall in line is bound to face significant pushback. The current sentiment indicates that the electorate is no longer willing to support agendas that exclude their voices. For the President to maintain political stability, he must bridge the divide between his top-down directives and the democratic expectations of the Kenyan people. True leadership requires balancing the desire for progress with a profound respect for the liberty of the governed.
