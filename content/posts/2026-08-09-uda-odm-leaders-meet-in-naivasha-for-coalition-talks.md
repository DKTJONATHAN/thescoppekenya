## The Naivasha Bargain: Whose Interests Are Actually Being Served? UDA and ODM leaders meet in Naivasha for coalition talks is the central subject of the update.

While the elite dine in the luxury of Naivasha, millions of Kenyans struggle to keep pace with the dwindling income of M-Pesa agents, whose annual commissions have plummeted to a record low of 112,244 shillings. This disconnect defines the current political climate.

As the political class gathers this August 9 for a three-day retreat, the agenda is clear: they are negotiating survival, not service. The UDA and ODM delegations, led by figures like Kimani Ichung'wah, Stewart Madzayo, and Wycliffe Oparanya, have traded the dust of the streets for the plush chairs of the Rift Valley. They call it a broad-based government, but in practice, it is a desperate insurance policy against public volatility. The optics of this alliance represent a complete surrender of the democratic mandate, replacing rigorous policy debate with a closed-door arrangement designed to ensure political continuity for the architects of the status quo.

### The Myth of the Fifty-Fifty Split

The proposal for a 50-50 power-sharing ratio, featuring demands for the deputy president position, is a mathematical exercise in exclusion. By formalizing this marriage of convenience, the architects of this coalition effectively sterilize the legislative process. When the government and the primary opposition party unify under one banner, the mechanisms of oversight vanish. Parliament ceases to be a house of review and becomes a rubber-stamp chamber for the executive wing.

Civil society actors have warned that this concentration of power leaves the average citizen without a voice. Without a robust opposition to challenge the cabinet appointments or the fiscal recklessness that saw [Ruto mandate local manufacturing for security footwear](https://zandani.co.ke/article/ruto-mandates-local-manufacturing-security-footwear) while basic consumer goods remain out of reach, the state becomes a closed shop. The focus has shifted from managing the cost of living to managing the political ambitions of those who believe that a seat at the table is the highest form of success. This is not about stabilizing the country; it is about cannibalizing the state to prevent any threat to the establishment.

### Vision 2060 and the Mirage of Progress

The mention of Vision 2060 in the Naivasha discourse provides a convenient intellectual veneer for what is essentially a campaign strategy meeting. Behind the closed doors, the conversation is fixated on the 2027 General Election. The NADCO report has been repurposed from a framework for national dialogue into a logistical manual for holding onto power. There is no talk of lowering taxes, reforming the public sector, or addressing the systemic failures that have left the economy fragile. Instead, the focus is on how to distribute influence and secure incumbency.

This political retreat ignores the reality that a government which avoids scrutiny eventually collapses under its own arrogance. By merging the interests of UDA and ODM, the leaders have created an environment where accountability is viewed as an inconvenience rather than a constitutional requirement. When the political class shares the same bed, the citizen is left to pay for the linens. These maneuvers are not the actions of leaders confident in their track record, but of a political elite terrified of an unpredictable electorate. The irony is that while they plan for a future decade of control, they ignore the current reality of a populace that is exhausted by the cycle of political posturing and unfulfilled promises.

If the primary goal of the Naivasha retreat is to secure the positions of the few at the expense of the many, what remains of the Kenyan promise to the millions left behind?
