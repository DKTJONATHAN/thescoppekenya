Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua faces mounting political instability within the United Democratic Alliance as internal rifts escalate in Nairobi. Allegations of sabotage from within his Mount Kenya stronghold currently threaten his influence and his standing within President William Ruto’s inner circle.

## The Strategy of Self-Sabotage

### The Myth of External Sabotage
Supporters of the Deputy President frequently claim that political moles are infiltrating the party to orchestrate his isolation. However, this narrative ignores the reality that his recent political maneuvers are inherently counterproductive. By relying on regional rhetoric and tribal posturing, Gachagua is not strengthening his position as a power broker. Instead, he is alienating key allies and creating political vulnerability, essentially doing the work of his rivals for them. His efforts to cultivate a regional base of power are inadvertently creating the exact distance from the presidency that his supporters claim to fear.

### The Misguided Loyalty of Followers
The base that remains fiercely loyal to the Deputy President continues to champion his aggressive stance as a necessary defense of regional interests. This perspective fails to account for how such rhetoric is viewed from the center of government. By framing his political identity around narrow geographical concerns, Gachagua is inadvertently providing President Ruto with a justification to shift focus toward broader national coalitions. His followers mistake his confrontational style for strength, while in reality, this posture serves only to diminish his leverage and provide his opponents with the political ammunition needed to sideline him.

### A Performance Over Policy
The focus on these internal power struggles has distracted the party from addressing pressing national governance issues. The political environment has shifted toward a choreographed performance where symbolic gestures are prioritized over strategic alliance-building. While Gachagua remains preoccupied with maintaining dominance over the Mount Kenya region, he is failing to recognize that his actions are thinning his support at the national level. By prioritizing local disputes, he is playing into a tactical trap that consolidates the President’s authority while simultaneously rendering the Deputy President’s own position increasingly precarious.
