## Institutional capture and the new regulatory guard

President William Ruto has moved to cement executive influence over the country's independent regulatory bodies, issuing a fresh set of appointments that prioritize long-term oversight control. By securing six-year terms for key board members across the policing, education, and civil service sectors, the administration is effectively bypassing the volatility of current political cycles to entrench its influence within the state's functional core.

### A trajectory of integration

The administration's governance style has evolved from the noisy, campaign-era promises of the early tenure into a quieter, more calculated method of institutional integration. This phase of board appointments signals a shift where the President is no longer looking for temporary surrogates, but for permanent fixtures who can provide stability to his policy objectives.

- August 2024: Mounting pressure from civil society organizations regarding transparency within the Teachers Service Commission prompted internal administrative audits that set the stage for current personnel changes.
- March 2025: Cabinet discussions regarding the professionalization of military veterans led to the formation of the new committee structure, which now finds its permanent leadership in the latest gazette notice.
- January 2026: The ongoing friction between the executive and independent authorities over budget autonomy forced the administration to prioritize the appointment of loyalist chairpersons at the Independent Policing Oversight Authority.

The inclusion of figures like Wilson Sossion into the Teachers Service Commission is perhaps the most telling indicator of this transition. Once a fierce adversary of state policy, Sossion's integration into the board suggests that the current administration favors coopting the most effective opposition voices into the engine room of government rather than leaving them to organize outside the tent. This mirrors a broader trend observed in recent weeks, where [Rigathi Gachagua gains political momentum](https://zandani.co.ke/article/rigathi-gachagua-gains-political-momentum-while-opposition-leadershi) as the administration simultaneously works to neutralize independent power centers through strategic, high-level placement of seasoned political operators.

### The arithmetic of control

The choice of tenure lengths reveals a distinct administrative hierarchy. The appointment of Duncan Oburu Ojwang at the Independent Policing Oversight Authority, alongside appointments to the Teachers Service Commission and the Public Service Commission, utilizes six-year terms to create a buffer against future political transitions. These institutions serve as the primary gatekeepers for law enforcement conduct, public education standards, and civil service integrity. By installing leadership that will outlast the current parliamentary term, the executive secures a legacy that survives beyond the next general election.

Conversely, the four-year term assigned to Major General William Karisa Shume at the Advisory Committee on Military Veterans suggests a different strategic intent. This sector remains closer to the direct tactical control of the security apparatus, where shorter cycles allow for a tighter leash and more frequent assessment of performance. While the public remains distracted by controversies like the [Freedom 250 concert fallout](https://zandani.co.ke/article/freedom-250-concert-artist-withdrawals-john-oliver-criticism), the state is systematically reinforcing its regulatory architecture.

### The outlook for oversight

These appointments do not merely fill vacant desks; they determine the administrative culture of these institutions for the remainder of the decade. The regulatory oversight implications are profound. When an administration chooses its own regulators to serve six-year terms, the distance between the governed and the governing institutions is drastically reduced. We are witnessing the solidification of a bureaucratic machine designed to withstand political shifts.

History shows that once the oversight boards are aligned with the executive agenda, the speed of policy implementation increases, though the efficacy of external checks on state power inevitably wanes. The state is betting on the idea that institutional loyalty will replace the need for constant crisis management. Future results will reveal if these appointments produce efficiency or merely silence the machines of democratic dissent.
