## The Eight-Hour Silence on Mombasa Road Kenya Power Schedules Maintenance Along Mombasa Road is the central subject of the update.

Kenya Power promises grid stability while simultaneously mandating an eight-hour industrial standstill for the primary manufacturing artery of the city. While the utility calls this essential maintenance, the ledger for businesses like Alliance Concrete tells a story of lost production cycles and ballooning overheads.

### The Cost of a Silent Factory

Today, August 22, 2026, the silence along Mombasa Road is not a sign of peace. It is an expensive void. From 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., the heavy machinery at Portland and Alliance Concrete facilities stands idle. In the concrete manufacturing sector, a forced eight-hour stoppage is not merely a pause. It is a disruption of chemical curing processes and a complete halt to pre-cast supply chains that operate on minute-by-minute efficiency. 

When a concrete plant loses power, the cost is not just the wasted electricity. It is the waste of raw materials already in the mixing drum, the labor costs for idle shifts, and the failure to meet delivery windows for construction sites across Nairobi. These companies operate on razor-thin margins where downtime is the enemy of profit. While the utility frames this as a necessary infrastructure upgrade, the economic reality is a direct levy on the productivity of the manufacturing sector.

### Tracking the Pattern of Disruption

This is not an isolated incident. Over the last 18 months, the Mombasa Road industrial zone has been subjected to a recurring cycle of scheduled interruptions. A look at the public record shows a pattern where maintenance notices are issued with a frequency that suggests the infrastructure is barely hanging on by a thread.

- January 2025: A series of three outages in six weeks forced a 12 percent drop in quarterly output for major masonry manufacturers in the corridor.
- October 2025: A weekend maintenance marathon resulted in prolonged restarts, causing gear fatigue in heavy-duty crushers that cost firms millions in unplanned repairs.
- August 2026: Today’s outage is the fourth significant disruption of the current year, effectively stripping a full day of productive potential from the annual industrial calendar.

Each notice arrives with the promise of future reliability, yet the history of these closures suggests that the grid remains in a state of permanent repair. The cumulative lost output from these outages is now a line item in the annual risk assessments for every major factory on the road.

### Balancing Necessity and Financial Reality

Kenya Power claims these shutdowns prevent catastrophic failures. However, the private sector is forced to absorb the massive financial impact of these preventive measures. For an industrial player, buying industrial-grade generators to bridge these gaps is a massive capital expenditure. Many firms are now choosing to invest in independent power solutions rather than relying on the grid. This creates a feedback loop: as companies move toward off-grid power, the utility loses its most reliable high-volume customers, further complicating its ability to fund the very upgrades needed to stop these outages.
