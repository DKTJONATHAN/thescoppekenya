## Software rules to save our phones Google sets new memory standards for Android apps to boost perfor is the central subject of the update.

Nairobi phone repair technicians are cheering after Google announced a 2027 policy requiring app developers to cap memory usage. This shift aims to stop bloated software from choking low-cost devices, effectively forcing apps to run smoothly on modest hardware.

Google recently confirmed that starting in 2027, all applications listed on the Play Store must meet strict memory and performance thresholds. By enforcing these efficiency standards, the company intends to punish resource-heavy apps that cause phones to freeze or overheat. Developers will be provided with new diagnostic toolkits to ensure their software respects the limitations of entry-level devices. This mandate targets the root cause of digital frustration for millions of Kenyans who rely on budget handsets, preventing developers from pushing code that demands more power than a standard device can provide.

### The view from Luthuli Avenue

On the bustling streets of Luthuli Avenue, the sentiment is one of cautious optimism. Samuel Maina, a seasoned technician who spends his days reviving sluggish handsets, says the change is long overdue. He explains that customers often bring in devices complaining that their phones have become unusable, but the hardware is rarely at fault. Instead, he finds that basic social media apps and essential utility tools are consuming excessive memory in the background. Maina believes that if developers are forced to streamline their code, he will see far fewer phones stuck in boot-loop cycles or suffering from persistent screen lag.

For those trading in the mtumba market, this policy acts as a form of consumer protection. Dealers who specialize in refurbished mid-range phones expect that devices with 3GB or 4GB of RAM will remain functional for two or three years longer than previously anticipated. One vendor noted that while shoppers often prioritize camera quality or storage capacity, the hidden tax of poor software optimization usually renders those phones obsolete within eighteen months. By setting a hard limit on how much memory a single app can demand, the industry is effectively extending the shelf life of affordable technology.

### Innovation versus accessibility

The tension between pushing for high-end features and ensuring global accessibility remains the core of this shift. Industry observers point out that Kenyan users often skip app updates or delete tools entirely because they occupy too much space and drain battery life. This creates a digital divide where only those with premium devices enjoy a seamless experience. If software is optimized for the next billion users rather than just the latest flagship handsets, the barrier to participating in the digital economy drops significantly. It is a reversal of the trend where phones were forced into retirement not by physical failure, but by software greed.

This 2027 timeline provides a generous window for developers to overhaul their existing products. It is a period that will likely see a decline in the prevalence of massive, poorly coded apps that act as bloatware.
