## Crude Logistics: Small Business Opportunity Along the Lokichar Route KPRL Secures Deal with Gulf Energy to Export Turkana Crude Oil is the central subject of the update.

The recent 25-year agreement between KPRL and Gulf Energy to revitalize Mombasa storage facilities by December 2026 marks a major pivot in Kenya’s oil sector. By moving away from a long-delayed, pipeline-only export strategy, the government is embracing a road and rail transport framework. This shift creates a tangible, decentralized opportunity for local transport firms, fleet managers, and roadside service providers to enter the Turkana crude supply chain.

### Moving Crude: A Shift Toward Localized Transport

Relying on road and rail networks to transport crude oil from Lokichar to the coast changes the logistics landscape entirely. Rather than waiting for a multi-billion dollar, state-led pipeline project, the sector is now pivoting toward existing infrastructure to move up to 50,000 barrels of oil daily. 

This transition favors agility over massive capital expenditures. Transporting petroleum products requires specialized tankers, strict adherence to safety protocols, and a reliable fleet. For local haulage companies, this is a chance to move beyond the volatility of seasonal agricultural freight. The key to securing a role in this chain is not just fleet size; it is operational compliance. Businesses must align their equipment, driver training, and hazardous materials certifications with the 2026 operational deadline. 

Small and medium enterprises (SMEs) that offer mid-mile logistics, secure refueling depots, or fleet management expertise are better positioned than ever to secure sub-contracts. Because this model relies on a distributed network of vehicles rather than a single fixed pipe, the logistics burden falls on local operators. If your business handles engine repairs, tire services, or diagnostic maintenance, the increased flow of heavy-duty vehicles along the North Rift corridor translates into a predictable, high-demand market.

### Supporting Services Along the Transit Corridor

Economic growth usually follows the route of heavy transport. The corridor between Lokichar and Mombasa will require a massive support ecosystem to keep trucks moving safely and efficiently. This extends well beyond the major city centers. 

Towns and stopovers along the route will experience a surge in demand for auxiliary services. Drivers will need consistent access to quality accommodation, secure parking, and reliable catering. Furthermore, there is an opening for SMEs to provide high-value support services. If your business provides digital fleet monitoring, cargo tracking, or logistics-adjacent support like warehouse security, you have roughly sixteen months to scale your operations. 

Modern transport is increasingly tech-driven. Just as global supply chains are leveraging software to optimize complex routes and reduce idle time, Kenyan haulers can gain a competitive advantage by adopting digital tracking tools. Integrating simple, effective fleet management software can help your business prove its reliability to larger partners, making you a preferred vendor when the Gulf Energy project hits full capacity.

### Practical Steps for Business Preparation

The December 2026 deadline is approaching, and the time for planning is now. Success in this new market will not come to those who wait for the first barrels to be loaded; it will go to those who have already streamlined their regulatory and operational standing.

First, prioritize compliance. Review the requirements set by the Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority (EPRA) for the handling and transport of hazardous materials. Being fully permitted is the baseline for entry. Once your paperwork is in order, focus on building partnerships. Attempting to secure direct, high-level contracts with energy conglomerates can be daunting for smaller firms. Instead, engage with regional business associations to identify larger logistics providers who may need to outsource local transit routes or specific support services. 

Networking is vital. By positioning yourself as a reliable, certified subcontractor, you can tap into the broader supply chain without needing to be the primary logistics partner. Monitor public procurement bulletins closely for updates on port handling services at the KOT II terminal. The transition to a road and rail export model represents a move toward a modular, scalable economy. For the prepared SME, being licensed, well-maintained, and ready to operate is the surest way to capture a share of the profit moving from the oil fields of Turkana to the coast of Mombasa.
