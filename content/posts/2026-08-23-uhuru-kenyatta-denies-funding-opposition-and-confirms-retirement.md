## Uhuru Kenyatta and the politics of distraction

Former President Uhuru Kenyatta has formally distanced himself from claims that he is bankrolling opposition movements, asserting at a memorial service in Kitale that his focus remains fixed on a quiet post-presidency life far removed from the halls of power.

The persistent friction between the current administration and its predecessor has created a political fog that obscures the urgent economic needs of the average Kenyan. While high-level rhetoric often focuses on allegations of shadow funding and legacy battles, the real cost of this ongoing tension is felt in the marketplace. When political discourse is dominated by the question of whether a former leader is plotting a return, legislative agendas suffer, and the focus shifts from fixing the cost of living to managing perceived political threats. This creates a state of policy paralysis where the government is perpetually in campaign mode, leaving critical economic reforms to gather dust.

### How does the shadow narrative freeze the economy?

Investor confidence relies heavily on predictability. When the national conversation is consumed by the friction between the state house and the retired president, markets react with caution. Foreign and local investors look for stability before committing capital to long-term projects. Instead, they see a climate where the administration is preoccupied with debunking theories about who is funding whom. This atmosphere of uncertainty acts as a hidden tax on the economy. When policy makers spend their energy debating political adversaries rather than streamlining trade or lowering production costs, the ripple effect reaches the small business owner in Eldoret or Nairobi. Commodity pricing often fluctuates during periods of intense political heat, as traders hedge against the risk of civil instability, eventually passing those costs directly to the consumer.

### Why service delivery suffers under political infighting

Government service delivery requires a laser focus on execution, yet the preoccupation with the 2027 elections has already begun to cannibalize the current mandate. Administrative resources that should be directed toward efficient public service are instead repurposed to counter political optics. The rivalry between the two camps forces the government to treat every policy choice as a potential wedge issue, leading to a hesitant, reactive style of governance. Just as the [President Ruto Hosts Africa Forward Summit as French Official Sparks Outrage in Kakamega](https://zandani.co.ke/article/president-ruto-hosts-africa-forward-summit-as-french-official-sparks) story illustrates how international optics can complicate local governance, this domestic feud creates a similar distraction. When the executive branch is trapped in a defensive posture against past leaders, the momentum required to drive infrastructural development or healthcare reform stalls. The public pays the price in delayed projects and neglected service sectors.

### The necessity of a neutral public space

Economic stability requires a clear separation between the past political order and the current executive agenda. Expert analysts point out that for the economy to regain its footing, there must be a cooling of the rhetoric surrounding retired leaders. A neutral public space allows the government to be judged on its own merits rather than through the lens of a manufactured rivalry. Without this detachment, the Kenyan public remains caught in a cycle of grievance politics that benefits neither the ruling coalition nor the opposition. True progress for the ordinary citizen demands a transition from the politics of personalities back to the politics of performance. Until the administration can step away from the shadows of 2027, the economic recovery remains a secondary priority to the survival of political narratives. A healthy democracy needs a constructive opposition, but it cannot function effectively when the national agenda is held hostage by the personal friction of its past and present leaders.

Focusing on personal political disputes prevents the nation from solving the systemic economic issues that affect every household.
