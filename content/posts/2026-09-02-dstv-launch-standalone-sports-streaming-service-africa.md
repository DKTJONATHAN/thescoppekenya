## DStv pivots to asset-light streaming

Canal+ is undertaking a radical restructuring of MultiChoice to address a ZAR 0.8 billion trading loss, positioning the future of African sports broadcasting on a standalone streaming model. By bypassing the hardware-heavy satellite infrastructure that has defined the company for decades, the group is betting on a lean, platform-centric architecture to secure its place in the mobile-first African market.

This strategic shift represents a fundamental evolution for the broadcaster. Rather than focusing on decoders and field installation teams, the company is pivoting toward a high-margin digital media framework. It is a calculated move to shed the capital-intensive burden of traditional utility models in favor of a flexible, scalable digital footprint.

### The economics of the mobile transition

For the Kenyan market, the R399 pricing pilot in South Africa offers a window into the future, suggesting a potential monthly threshold near KES 3,000. This pricing is designed to capture the upwardly mobile middle class while incentivizing existing DStv Premium subscribers to transition toward a more agile digital experience. Kenya’s deep integration with mobile money infrastructure provides a natural advantage here. By embedding M-Pesa billing directly into the streaming interface, MultiChoice can bypass the friction of manual satellite renewals, creating a frictionless transaction environment for millions of mobile money users.

The underlying math of this transition is driven by the reality of 60 million mobile broadband subscriptions across the continent. While satellite infrastructure remains useful in regions with limited connectivity, the rapid expansion of 4G networks-reaching 84.8 percent in major urban hubs-makes high-definition sports streaming a viable, dish-free reality. By removing the physical hardware layer, the company eliminates significant logistical overhead and depreciation costs. The goal is to shift from asset management to user acquisition, mirroring the metrics used by global tech giants to measure engagement and growth.

### Platform-first over content-first

Canal+ is actively dismantling the closed ecosystem that once anchored MultiChoice’s market dominance. The previous strategy relied on locking viewers into a proprietary hardware chain, but the new mandate prioritizes platform agility. This approach reflects a broader regional shift where audience retention is increasingly untethered from traditional media gatekeepers. Just as digital creators have discovered that direct audience engagement supersedes institutional branding, MultiChoice is moving toward a model where value is derived from platform accessibility rather than the physical ownership of a broadcast receiver.

This transition from a content-only focus-traditionally defined by high-cost rights for leagues like the English Premier League-to a platform-first strategy reshapes how investment flows within the organization. Resources are being diverted toward UI optimization, cloud server capacity, and sophisticated data analytics. By standardizing the app experience, MultiChoice gains the ability to push software updates and localized payment prompts across multiple markets simultaneously. This eliminates the need for regional technical teams to handle manual interventions, allowing for a more cohesive and responsive service.

### The regional forecast

As the platform-first strategy heads toward a Kenyan pilot in late 2026, the local market should prepare for a period of intense competitive pricing. Telecom providers, who once viewed satellite offerings as peripheral, are set to become essential distribution partners through the bundling of data and sports passes. Under French ownership, MultiChoice is adopting a more rigid, performance-based evaluation of its regional operations. If this streaming-only pivot achieves its goals, the physical satellite dish will quickly transition from a standard household feature to a relic of the early twenty-first century. Over the next eighteen months, expect a steady thinning of the traditional satellite footprint as mobile data costs drop and digital-first habits take firm root across the continent.
