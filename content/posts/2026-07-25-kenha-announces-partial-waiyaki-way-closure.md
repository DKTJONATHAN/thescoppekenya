## Nairobi Transit Shifts as Major Arteries Face Partial Shutdown KeNHA announces month long partial closure of Waiyaki Way is the central subject of the update.

The Kenya National Highways Authority will close specific Mombasa-bound lanes on Waiyaki Way from July 31 to August 31, 2026, forcing thousands of daily commuters to find alternative routes as part of a high-stakes infrastructure upgrade.

This month-long disruption centers on the vital transit stretch between the Global Trade Centre and the Villarosa Kempinski. The maintenance is a critical component of a Ksh4.5 billion modernization project aimed at reinforcing the structural integrity of Nairobi's primary commercial corridor. By focusing work on this specific segment, engineers intend to address long-standing drainage and pavement degradation that has previously caused localized flooding during heavy rains.

The logistics of the operation require a reconfiguration of traffic flow that will ripple through Westlands, Kangemi, and the Riverside district. Motorists heading toward the city center or Jomo Kenyatta International Airport from the Nairobi-Nakuru Highway are expected to face significant delays. Traffic marshals and police units are already undergoing briefings to manage the influx of vehicles into secondary arteries, as the main route narrows to accommodate heavy construction machinery.

KeNHA officials justify the timeline by citing the urgency of completing the maintenance before the peak of the next rainy season. While commuters often view such closures as mere inconveniences, the authority maintains that this investment is necessary to prevent more costly, emergency-level repairs in the future. The project aims to improve the longevity of the asphalt surface and ensure that traffic throughput remains consistent despite the increasing vehicle volume in the capital.

Economic analysts point to the hidden cost of such projects, noting that the disruption will likely translate into a substantial loss of commuter man-hours. For businesses located along the ABC Place to Westlands strip, the roadwork threatens to slow down supply chain movements and reduce physical customer access for the duration of August. The aggregate impact on productivity is difficult to quantify, yet local chambers of commerce have expressed concern that the construction period coincides with several high-profile corporate events planned for the district.

Public reaction remains mixed, as residents of Kitisuru, Loresho, and Mountain View consider the feasibility of utilizing feeder roads like Lower Kabete and Kyuna to bypass the gridlock. The construction sector, however, remains optimistic about the project's technical specifications, emphasizing that the reinforced materials used in this phase will significantly reduce the frequency of surface patching required in the surrounding Parklands and Highridge zones.

Safety remains the primary justification for the shift in traffic management.
