### Orchestrating an Unconventional Hospital Gig

The impetus for this extraordinary occasion was 27-year-old Tim Berry, a Bruyère Health patient whose medical condition prevented him from attending a desired concert. Chaplain Roshene Lawson saw an opportunity beyond her typical duties. Convinced by music's therapeutic influence and the importance of personal interests, Lawson embraced the challenge of bringing the live experience to Berry. She initiated a unique collaboration, reaching out to Ottawa's famed Dominion Tavern and the local rock outfit, The Scepter, formed in 2018. This partnership ultimately saw a fifth-floor hospital boardroom converted into a dynamic, albeit temporary, concert space. The sheer audacity of a heavy metal band performing inside a usually tranquil clinical setting underscored a significant imaginative leap in patient engagement.

### Profound Compassion Meets Rock 'n' Roll

This initiative served as a powerful testament to profound compassion, impacting everyone involved. Josh Collins and The Scepter members channeled their full energy into the unconventional venue, delivering an authentic and powerful performance for Berry and a small gathering of other patients and staff. For Tim Berry, the transformation was evident. His joy during the set was undeniable, finally realizing a dream previously unattainable due to his medical limitations. Scott McClinton, owner of the Dominion Tavern, present at the event, found it deeply moving, highlighting the universal resonance of seeing a personal aspiration brought to fruition. Lawson's persistent efforts, combined with the cooperative spirit uniting Bruyère Health, the local tavern, and the musicians, emphasize an evolving understanding that genuine care transcends mere medical treatments.
