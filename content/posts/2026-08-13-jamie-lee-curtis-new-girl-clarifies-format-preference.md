## Jamie Lee Curtis Details Her Comedy Format Preference, Not a Dislike for 'New Girl' "Jamie Lee Curtis Clarifies 'New Girl' Format Preference is the central subject of the update.

Veteran actress Jamie Lee Curtis has clarified her recent remarks about the acclaimed comedy 'New Girl', asserting her admiration for the series itself. Her comments specifically targeted the production format, not the show's quality, highlighting a professional preference for multicamera productions.

Hollywood icon Jamie Lee Curtis took to social media this week to set the record straight regarding previous statements perceived as critical of the popular Fox sitcom, 'New Girl'. Curtis, 67, asserted her deep appreciation for the show's quality, clarifying that her earlier remarks were solely focused on her professional preference for multicamera comedy formats over single-camera productions due to the unique energy of a live audience. The clarification comes amidst promotion for her new multicamera series.

The initial comments that sparked discussion were made during NBCUniversal's Summer Backlot Experience. While discussing her upcoming NBC series, 'Newlyweds', a multicamera comedy, Curtis expressed a performer's perspective on the "silent" nature of single-camera productions. Her statements were interpreted by some as a direct critique of 'New Girl', leading to a wave of online speculation about her true feelings toward the beloved series where she had a memorable guest role.

It is important to note that Curtis herself was part of the 'New Girl' universe. She famously portrayed Joan Day, the eccentric mother of lead character Jess Day, played by Zooey Deschanel. Her appearances, often alongside Rob Reiner who played her on-screen ex-husband Bob Day, were well-received by fans. In her recent clarification, Curtis emphasized that 'New Girl' is a "great show" and "fantastic," dispelling any notions that her technical critique translated into a personal dislike for the program or her co-stars.

### The Dynamics of Comedy Formats

Curtis's distinction between enjoying a show and having a format preference underscores a key operational difference in television production. Single-camera comedies, like 'New Girl', are shot more akin to films, scene by scene, often without a live studio audience. This allows for cinematic aesthetics and more intricate visual storytelling. Multicamera shows, conversely, are typically filmed on a soundstage in front of a live audience, providing immediate feedback from laughter and applause. For performers like Curtis, this live interaction is a vital element of the comedic craft, influencing timing and delivery.

Her passion for the multicamera format is central to her involvement with 'Newlyweds', which she is both an executive producer and a recurring guest star on. The new NBC series, slated to premiere on October 23 at 8:30 p.m. East Africa Time, features Téa Leoni and Tim Daly in lead roles. Curtis's promotional efforts for 'Newlyweds' at the Backlot Experience were the context for her original remarks, aiming to highlight the unique appeal and challenges of the multicamera setup, particularly for actors who thrive on direct audience engagement.

The social media clarification was accompanied by a warm throwback photo of Curtis, Deschanel, and Reiner from their time on the 'New Girl' set. This gesture cemented her admiration for the show and its cast, reframing the narrative from a perceived criticism to a professional discussion about artistic preference. It reminded audiences that industry comments, particularly from seasoned professionals, often carry a nuanced technical perspective beyond simple likes or dislikes.
