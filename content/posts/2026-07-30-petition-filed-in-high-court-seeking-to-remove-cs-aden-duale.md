## The Duale Doctrine and the Death of Oversight

A formal petition filed at the High Court now seeks to strip Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale of his office, challenging his recent conduct in Mandera County. The move forces a confrontation between ministerial prerogative and the statutory authority of independent oversight institutions.

The petitioners allege that remarks made by the Health Cabinet Secretary on July 20, 2026, in Lafey Constituency crossed the red line of Article 33(2). This constitutional provision explicitly excludes hate speech, incitement to violence, and advocacy of hatred from the protection of freedom of expression. By invoking these legal standards, the court filing moves beyond political grievance and into the domain of constitutional fitness for public office. The core of the matter is not merely the rhetoric itself, but the brazen dismissal of the subsequent summons issued by the National Cohesion and Integration Commission (NCIC).

### The Shield of Executive Immunity

The NCIC exists to act as a buffer against the degradation of public discourse by those in power. When a Cabinet Secretary treats a summons from this body as a suggestion rather than a mandate, the entire architecture of the National Cohesion and Integration Act of 2008 faces a stress test. The silence from the executive branch on this standoff is deafening. There is no official rebuke, no internal inquiry, and no indication that the Cabinet views the defiance of a constitutional body as a breach of the ministerial code of conduct.

This institutional paralysis suggests that the Cabinet operates as a closed loop. By failing to hold one of its own to account for ignoring a summons, the government effectively signals that ministerial immunity acts as a bypass for legal compliance. If the executive branch collectively treats an NCIC subpoena as optional, the commission is reduced to a hollow vessel. We see similar institutional decay elsewhere, such as when Harambee Stars players reject team hotel conditions despite persistent poor performance, highlighting how systemic neglect of standards eventually erodes the integrity of entire sectors. When leaders ignore the rules, they invite everyone else to treat them as voluntary suggestions.

### Precedent and the Erosion of Checks

The legal challenge hinges on whether a Cabinet Secretary can claim a higher standing than a commission established to protect the republic from ethnic fragmentation. If the High Court dismisses the petition, it implicitly sanctions a new norm: that state officers are untouchable as long as they maintain the confidence of their appointing authority, regardless of their compliance with other oversight mechanisms. This is a dangerous trajectory. The stability of the state rests on the belief that no official is above the mandate of constitutional bodies.

Should this defiance stand, the NCIC will have no teeth for the rest of this administration. The implications for the Rule of Law are clear. The government is currently navigating a period of intense volatility, with fiscal strain and the purchasing power of citizens eroding daily, as seen in the recent patterns at the Nairobi Securities Exchange. When economic anxiety meets political inflammatory rhetoric that goes unchecked, the result is rarely stability. The Cabinet Secretary’s refusal to appear is not a trivial procedural matter. It is an assertion that the executive branch has outgrown the need for public accountability.

This standoff does not happen in a vacuum. It forces the judiciary to define the boundaries of executive power in real time. The court must now decide if the office of the Cabinet Secretary is a position of public trust subject to the laws of the land, or a fortress where individuals can retreat from the consequences of their own words. If a state officer can walk away from a statutory obligation with total impunity, what remains of the constitutional safeguards meant to keep the peace?
