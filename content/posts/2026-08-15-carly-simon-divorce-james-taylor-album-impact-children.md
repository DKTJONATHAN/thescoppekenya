Eighteen years since her previous release, Carly Simon's new album, 'Comes in Waves,' delves into the profound echoes of her well-documented 1983 separation from James Taylor. With the track 'Mother of Pearl,' Simon initiates a heartfelt conversation with her daughter, Sarah, examining how the fracturing of their family unit has endured.

Imagine a parent reaching out through song, decades after a pivotal family event, seeking to explain, understand, or heal. This deeply personal gesture anchors Carly Simon's latest album, 'Comes in Waves,' marking her first studio release in nearly two decades. This highly anticipated collection presents listeners with more than fresh tunes; the iconic singer-songwriter offers a candid emotional exploration, particularly evident in the track 'Mother of Pearl.' Here, Simon directly addresses her daughter, Sarah 'Sally' Maria, reflecting on the lasting impact of her well-known separation from fellow music legend James Taylor.

Once, the union of Carly Simon and James Taylor shone as a celebrated pairing within music royalty. Their paths converged in 1971, leading to a Manhattan wedding in 1972. For eleven years, their lives and artistic endeavors were deeply interwoven; Simon, notably, lent her voice to some of Taylor's recordings, contributing backing vocals to tracks like an acoustic version of "You Can Close Your Eyes" and "Devoted to You" in 1978. Yet, the combined strains of public life and personal challenges eventually led to their separation, culminating in their 1983 split. While the public perceived it as the dramatic end of a fairytale romance, for their young children, Sarah and Ben, it profoundly reshaped their reality. Simon herself revealed in a 2015 interview with PEOPLE magazine that communication between her and Taylor remained non-existent, highlighting the persistent chasm.

### 'Mother of Pearl' - A Parent's Long-Awaited Conversation

With the arrival of 'Comes in Waves,' Simon revisits that crucial juncture in her life. She approaches it not with the typical fanfare surrounding celebrity separations, but with a candid vulnerability offered directly to her child. 'Mother of Pearl,' positioned as the fifth track on the 12-song collection, invites listeners into the intimate act of a mother explaining the inexplicable for her daughter. Sarah was merely seven years old when her parents' union ended. This song serves as a retrospective gaze, perhaps an explanation, a means of processing, or even a subtle apology for a path that indelibly shaped a young life.

The resonance of this song extends far beyond the celebrated lives of the Simon-Taylor family. It speaks to a universal truth: while adults navigate the complexities of marriage and separation, children often bear the deepest, quietest wounds. Simon's vulnerability, candidly revealed in her lyrics, underscores that understanding the aftermath, even decades later, is vital for healing and progress.
