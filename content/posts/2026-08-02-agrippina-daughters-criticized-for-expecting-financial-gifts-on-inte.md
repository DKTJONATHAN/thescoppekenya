Recent discussions in Nairobi have centered on the growing trend of women soliciting expensive gifts and extravagant outings on International Women’s Day. Critics argue this behavior misinterprets the holiday, shifting the focus from genuine female empowerment toward a demand for financial subsidies from men.

## Agrippina Daughters criticized for expecting financial gifts on International Women's Day

### The high cost of empowerment
The cultural shift surrounding March 8 has become a point of contention across the capital. While the day is internationally recognized to celebrate the social, economic, and political achievements of women, a growing segment of the population has redefined the holiday as an opportunity for financial extraction. Men are increasingly facing pressure to fund lavish dinners and luxury purchases, a trend that critics argue undermines the very spirit of the occasion. Observers maintain that the holiday should be a time to recognize genuine progress rather than performative materialism.

### A historical comparison to manipulation
Commentators have begun using the label Daughters of Agrippina to describe those who view their relationships through a strictly transactional lens. Referencing the ancient figure known for her calculated and manipulative rise to power, the term mocks women who weaponize the holiday to demand financial support from their partners. The critique suggests that using the concept of empowerment as a mask for greed is a low-voltage endeavor, implying that such behavior lacks the intellectual spark of true independence. Much like high-stakes geopolitical maneuvering, this approach treats every social interaction as a move requiring a heavy financial payout.

### Refocusing the narrative
The pushback against this entitlement highlights a desire to return to the foundational roots of the women's movement. Proponents of this view argue that true female solidarity is built on shared professional goals and individual advancement, rather than on the monetary value of gifts received. This perspective emphasizes that genuine success is dictated by personal hard work and industry. For many, the modern expectation of a holiday payday is a distraction from the substantial, ongoing efforts required to dismantle systemic barriers to equality. The consensus among these critics is clear: waiting for external sponsorship to validate one's status contradicts the principles of self-sufficiency.
