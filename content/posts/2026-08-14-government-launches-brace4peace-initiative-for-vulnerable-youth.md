## Beyond the Workshop: Are 450 Youth the State's New Election Shield?

Deep in the dust-caked streets of Isiolo and the humid back-alleys of Kilifi, the government is betting that a welding torch or a tailoring kit can replace the Molotov cocktail. While the state presents the BRACE4PEACE initiative as a path to prosperity, the math suggests a different priority: securing the peace-and perhaps the ballot box-long before the first voter hits the 2027 polls.

### The Arithmetic of Influence

The program claims to transform the lives of 450 vulnerable youth, pulling them from the fringes of extremist recruiters and street-level crime. By slicing this cohort into neat packets of 90 participants per county-specifically targeting Nairobi, Kilifi, Kisumu, Isiolo, and Samburu-the state is testing a hyper-localized social containment strategy. These regions are rarely selected at random; they are historical flashpoints where economic desperation has traditionally been mobilized for political muscle.

Critics are already asking if this is empowerment or merely a high-stakes insurance policy. Dennis Elnino, a youth organizer who has seen dozens of similar projects fade into obscurity once the ballot boxes are tucked away, is skeptical. He notes that while the promise of vocational training is sweet, the timing is sour. To him, the training feels like a temporary buffer designed to keep potential agitators busy and dependent on state-sanctioned programs while the political machinery gears up for the 2027 cycle. If the funding dries up as soon as the winner is declared, these youth will be left with a certificate of attendance but no capital to start a business.

### Security Rebranded as Skill-Building

Dr. Raymond Omollo, representing the architects of this plan, has been careful to frame the initiative as a pragmatic marriage between internal security and economic development. During the fifth steering committee meeting on peacebuilding, the justification was clear: extremism and crime do not thrive in vacuum-sealed economies. By linking vocational training directly to the security docket, the government is signaling that it no longer views youth unemployment as a social service problem, but as a direct threat to national stability.

For those on the ground, this alignment is unsettling. It suggests that if a young person stops being a 'risk' to the electoral process, the interest in their economic growth might evaporate. This is a recurring tension in local politics, much like the current friction surrounding fuel costs where John Mbadi must balance the threat of nationwide strikes against the cold reality of fiscal austerity. Just as citizens worry about the fluctuating pump prices that erode their daily earnings, these 450 participants are learning that their primary value to the state is their potential to stay quiet.

### The 2027 Horizon

The long-term sustainability of BRACE4PEACE remains the elephant in the room. In regions like Samburu, where economic opportunities are as dry as the landscape, a few months of training cannot undo decades of structural exclusion. Youth leaders are questioning why the government is so eager to invest in this specific cohort now, rather than scaling up permanent infrastructure that would outlive any single political regime.

There is a palpable sense that once the election dust settles, the machinery of BRACE4PEACE will be dismantled, leaving the participants to drift back into the same vulnerabilities they faced before the state came knocking. If the goal is truly to empower a generation, the initiative needs to look beyond the immediate electoral calendar. Otherwise, this becomes another chapter in a history of programs that treat the youth as shields during the heat of competition, only to abandon them once the seat of power is occupied. As the nation grapples with external pressures-like the rising shipping surcharges hitting our ports-the internal pressure of managing a disillusioned youth population remains the state's most pressing, and perhaps most cynical, project.
