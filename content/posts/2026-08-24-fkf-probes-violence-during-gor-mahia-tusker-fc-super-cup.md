### A Policy of Calculated Confusion

The current safety policy maintained by the federation reads like a document designed for a controlled exhibition rather than the high-stakes reality of local football. It fails to define clear boundaries between the responsibilities of hired private guards and the public police force. In a professional environment, standard operating procedures dictate that security details remain localized to the pitch perimeter to prevent movement. Instead, Nyayo Stadium saw a complete overlap where public police and private staff engaged in redundant, often contradictory, maneuvers.

Security consultants familiar with the venue have long pointed out that the facility lacks a tiered response plan. When the pitch was stormed, the absence of a graduated force policy meant that the immediate fallback was the use of tear gas in a confined space. This reflex action ignored the risks to thousands of spectators and the critical safety of journalists tasked with reporting on the event. ### The Missing Protection for the Press

Accredited journalists operate in a legal gray zone at Kenyan stadiums. While the FKF requires media to obtain credentials for access, those same credentials provide zero protection when the security environment collapses. There are no specific safety zones or exit protocols outlined for the media in the event of civil unrest on the turf. Consequently, when security officers decided to clear the pitch, reporters were treated as obstacles to be cleared rather than neutral observers to be shielded.

The disparity between the FKF safety manual and the ground reality is stark. The manual suggests a professional, tiered approach to stadium security, yet the execution remains anchored in archaic, aggressive crowd control tactics that do not distinguish between a fan encroaching on the grass and a cameraman standing on the sidelines. There is no training requirement for security personnel that emphasizes the preservation of press freedoms or the safety of non-combatants during these disturbances.
