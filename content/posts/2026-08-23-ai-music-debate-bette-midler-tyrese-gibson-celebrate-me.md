The digital soundscape hums with a question deeper than typical celebrity spats: what truly constitutes art? This fundamental query comes into sharp focus as music icons Bette Midler and Tyrese Gibson voice differing views on artificial intelligence in music. Their public exchange follows the viral success of IngaRose's "Celebrate Me," sparking a wider discussion on accountability for creativity’s future.

Legendary entertainer Bette Midler recently initiated a passionate discourse on Instagram, articulating profound alarm over the rise of AI-generated music. For Midler, whose career is built on raw talent and heartfelt delivery, the concept of algorithms crafting hits strikes at the core of human artistry. She spoke plainly, characterizing the use of AI in music production as nothing less than "cultural genocide." This stark assertion highlights her conviction that such technology threatens to erase the unique contributions and struggles that define human creative expression. Her position resonates with many who fear a diluted artistic landscape, where the soul of music might yield to synthetic efficiency.

Midler is not alone in her apprehension. Several prominent artists have expressed similar concerns, emphasizing the critical need to preserve the integrity of human-made music. Natasha Bedingfield, known for her evocative vocals, and Grammy winner India Arie have both publicly aligned with Midler's perspective. Their collective worry centers on the potential erosion of traditional artistry and the diminishing value of human talent. This sentiment echoes through creative communities globally, including here in Kenya, where cultural authenticity remains deeply cherished.

### "Celebrate Me" and Tyrese's Counter-Chorus

On the other side of this intensifying conversation about AI and music stands actor and singer Tyrese Gibson. He has emerged as a vocal defender of "Celebrate Me" by IngaRose. The track, despite its algorithmic origins, has undeniably captivated listeners, accumulating over 23 million streams on Spotify and attracting 1.1 million monthly listeners for IngaRose. Its commercial success serves as a compelling case study for the growing impact of AI-generated music. Gibson’s argument is rooted in the emotional connection people feel to the song, irrespective of its creator. He passionately questioned on Instagram whether human artists inherently possess a "superior" ability to craft music that resonates. If a song moves you, its source should be secondary, he suggested.

Gibson's viewpoint challenges the traditional gatekeepers of artistic legitimacy. He emphasizes that in an era where music consumption is driven by accessibility and emotional impact, the method of creation might hold less significance for the listener than the experience itself. His defense suggests a shift in how we define artistic value, moving beyond the traditional human-centric model. He envisions one that embraces new technological frontiers, provided the output genuinely connects with an audience.

### Who Guides Kenya's Creative Future and Artistic Standards?

This global celebrity exchange quickly moves beyond individual opinions. It forces us to confront a more profound question: who is truly accountable for setting ethical and artistic standards in this evolving landscape? Does the responsibility lie with the AI developers crafting these sophisticated tools? With the platforms that host and monetize AI-generated content? The artists themselves who choose to embrace or reject these technologies? Or the consumers whose preferences ultimately drive demand? The answers are complex and multifaceted.

For Kenyan musicians and the broader creative industries, this dialogue about AI carries significant weight. Issues of intellectual property (IP) become paramount as AI tools learn from and potentially replicate existing works. Safeguarding cultural authenticity - the distinct sounds, rhythms, and narratives that define Kenyan music - is also a pressing concern. As technology progresses, there is a vital need to ensure that local artists are protected, fairly compensated, and empowered to innovate rather than being overshadowed. Kenyan artists, much like the wider populace encouraged to focus on individual initiative rather than relying solely on politicians for progress, must actively engage in shaping these discussions to secure their future.

### Navigating the New Soundscape

The clash between Midler and Gibson, alongside the success of "Celebrate Me," underscores that the conversation around AI in music is ongoing. It is a dialogue demanding collaboration and foresight from all stakeholders to navigate the exciting, yet challenging, possibilities. As we look ahead, the coming years will undoubtedly see continued discussions around regulations, artist rights, and the very definition of creativity in a world where machines can compose and perform. The challenge, particularly for emerging markets like Kenya, will be to embrace innovation while fiercely protecting the invaluable human element that has always been the heart of our vibrant musical heritage.
