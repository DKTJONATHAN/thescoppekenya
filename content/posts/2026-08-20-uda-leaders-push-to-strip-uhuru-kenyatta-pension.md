## Political Leverage and the Retirement Benefits Dispute Western Kenya UDA leaders push to strip Uhuru Kenyatta of pension is the central subject of the update.

Western Kenya political leaders have launched a coordinated campaign to strip former President Uhuru Kenyatta of his retirement benefits, framing the move as a necessary response to his alleged involvement in active politics.

This high-stakes maneuver was unveiled during a UDA-affiliated forum in Kakamega County on August 20. By challenging the legal standing of Kenyatta under the Presidential Retirement Benefits Act, regional leaders are signaling a shift in national power dynamics. The move effectively weaponizes state entitlements to enforce political neutrality among retired heads of state.

The strategy serves as a calculated down payment to secure the political future of Western Kenya. Regional powerbrokers, including key figures from Kakamega and Bungoma, have openly linked their support for President William Ruto’s 2027 re-election to specific national concessions. The primary demand involves a clear path toward the Deputy Presidency in the next administration and a formal endorsement for a Luhya candidate to occupy the top office by 2032.

By targeting Kenyatta, these leaders are attempting to extract political capital from the current administration while simultaneously weakening the influence of the former regime. This approach reflects a broader trend where regional blocs leverage parliamentary procedure to dismantle the political machinery of their predecessors. The financial implications are significant, as setting a precedent for stripping presidential pensions could permanently alter the stability of the executive transition process in Kenya.

### The Strategy of Regional Bargaining

The push for a parliamentary motion represents more than a personal grievance against the former President. It functions as a litmus test for the loyalty of the Western Kenya voting bloc, which has pledged a delivery of four million votes for the current government in the upcoming 2027 cycle. Legislators driving this agenda argue that the law is clear regarding the prohibition of partisan activities by retired leaders.

Internal dissent, however, exists within the regional leadership structure. While figures such as John Waluke and Ayub Savula have championed the pension withdrawal, some local administrators and governors remain cautious. There is an underlying concern that such aggressive tactics could alienate voters who perceive the move as an escalation of petty grievances. Despite these reservations, the dominant faction continues to treat the pension issue as a strategic bargaining chip to maintain pressure on the executive branch.

### Long-term Succession and Political Precedents

Securing the 2032 presidency is the central motivation behind the current regional maneuvering. Leaders from the Mulembe nation are positioning their support for President Ruto as a conditional arrangement rather than a blank check. The demand for the Deputy Presidency is being presented as the primary condition for delivering the promised four million votes in the next election.

The political impact of this move extends beyond the immediate target. If the motion gains traction in the National Assembly, it would establish a new, precarious norm for Kenyan governance. Using the threat of financial divestment to control the political activities of former leaders invites a cycle of retaliatory measures. This atmosphere of political volatility remains a critical factor for the stability of upcoming national elections.
