## Farmers Demand Justice Over Fertilizer Fraud DCI Seizes 3,500 Bags of Counterfeit Fertiliser in Uasin Gishu is the central subject of the update.

"If the soil is poisoned, our children starve by December," is the bitter refrain echoing across the villages of Soy and Moiben. For thousands of families who invested their last savings into the current planting season, the news of counterfeit agricultural inputs has morphed from a police report into a personal existential threat.

The Directorate of Criminal Investigations has confirmed the seizure of 3,500 bags of suspected counterfeit fertiliser in a coordinated raid on premises in Kabenes, Uasin Gishu. This operation, which also led to the arrest of three suspects, follows an earlier recovery of 624 bags of similar illicit product in Moiben. Detectives are currently tracking additional members of a distribution network that appears to have compromised the integrity of the regional supply chain. While the authorities focus on mapping the criminal infrastructure, the actual victims are the small-scale farmers who now look at their maize fields with intense dread, wondering if the bags they purchased for thousands of shillings are merely sacks of worthless filler.

### Voices from the Furrow

The anxiety on the ground is palpable, replacing the usual optimism of the agricultural cycle. Samuel Koech, a farmer in Soy, points to his stunted stalks and shakes his head, noting that he poured his entire capital into a brand he trusted. He feels cheated not just of his money, but of his time, fearing that by the time the fake fertiliser is identified in the soil, the window for a successful harvest will have long closed.

In Moiben, Elizabeth Chebet shares a similar sentiment, stating that the promise of a bountiful yield has been replaced by the cold reality of potential debt. She highlights that for many, these inputs were bought on credit, and with the harvest looking bleak, she sees no path toward repayment.

Meanwhile, local community leaders are questioning the ease with which these materials entered the market. One village elder observes that the culprits have turned the Breadbasket of Kenya into a hunting ground, exploiting the desperate need for affordable inputs to distribute poison under the guise of progress. They are calling for more than just arrests; they are demanding a complete audit of the distribution networks that allow such large volumes of contraband to reach the rural interior.

### The Sabotage of National Security

The impact of this fraud extends far beyond the borders of Uasin Gishu. Each bag of fake product serves as a direct blow to national food security, threatening to reduce the expected output from a region that feeds the country. Agricultural experts warn that the use of adulterated inputs degrades the soil long-term, creating a cycle of dependency and low productivity that will take years to reverse. As investigators widen their search for the masterminds behind the Kabenes and Moiben caches, the focus is shifting to how these products moved through standard supply chains. The DCI has promised that the net is closing on the remaining actors, but for the people of Uasin Gishu, the damage is already visible in the discolouration of their crops. The community remains on edge, watching the progress of the investigation while wondering if the season can be salvaged or if this betrayal will define their economic year. Just as people move on from the noise of the [12th AMVCA](https://zandani.co.ke/article/don-julio-celebrates-film-excellence-at-amvca) to find their own stability, the farmers are looking for a clear sign that their hard work still holds value in a market suddenly flooded with deception.

The mood in the valley is one of grim anticipation, as the fields wait to reveal the full extent of the harvest failure.
