### The Cost of a Gusty Afternoon

In the bustling trading hubs of Kakamega and Nakuru, the structural integrity of a stall often rests on little more than frayed ropes and wooden poles. When the forecast calls for winds surpassing 25 knots, the danger is not just a messy workspace, it is the potential loss of a day or two of capital. Market traders spend their mornings reinforcing corrugated iron sheets and securing plastic tarps, knowing that a single powerful gust can dismantle a livelihood in seconds. 

This environment highlights how fragile the informal economy remains when faced with climate shifts. While many talk about the grand scale of national development, the reality for the person selling sukuma wiki on a roadside is dictated by the velocity of the air. These traders are essentially betting their daily earnings against the sky. For the boda-boda riders waiting at corners in towns like Bungoma or Eldoret, these winds represent a different kind of risk. Navigating a motorcycle through crosswinds while carrying passengers or heavy cargo is a precarious exercise. Riders report that days with high wind speeds often lead to lower earnings, as they have to drive slower and exercise extreme caution, effectively cutting their daily trip numbers in half just to stay upright on the road.
