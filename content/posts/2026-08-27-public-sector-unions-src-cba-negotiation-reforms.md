## The Negotiation Bottleneck Threatening Your Public Services

Public service trade unions met in Nairobi yesterday to challenge the current framework for Collective Bargaining Agreements. This standoff with the Salaries and Remuneration Commission creates a fiscal uncertainty that could soon disrupt daily operations across government offices, schools, and hospitals.

### Why are unions and the SRC currently clashing?

At the center of this dispute is the right to determine how salary negotiations occur. Trade unions argue that their current role in the Collective Bargaining Agreement process is too limited, leaving workers without a fair say in their welfare. They are pushing for direct, meaningful participation in every stage of these discussions to ensure that pay packages reflect the rising cost of living.

Conversely, the Salaries and Remuneration Commission, led by Chairperson Sammy Chepkwony, views the current system as a contributor to the frequent industrial action that paralyzes the country. The Commission is actively reviewing the negotiation process, aiming to centralize control to prevent the recurring strikes that often follow deadlocked talks. While the SRC frames this as a move toward stability and fiscal prudence, the unions see it as an attempt to sideline their bargaining power. This fundamental disagreement over who holds the pen during negotiations has brought the entire system to a standstill.

### How does this affect the flow of your salary funds?

For the ordinary Kenyan, this power struggle is more than just a bureaucratic debate in a Nairobi boardroom. When negotiations stall, the "money pipeline" slows down. County government staff are already feeling the brunt of this delay, with many reporting irregular or late salary payments. These workers form the backbone of service delivery in the regions. When their pay is caught in a tug-of-war between union demands and commission reforms, the ripple effect reaches the taxpayer.

If a nurse or a teacher is not paid, services stop. If a county accountant is trapped in a dispute over salary structures, payments for essential supplies are held up, forcing schools and health facilities to operate with depleted resources. The current friction means that even when funds are available, the administrative machinery required to release them is stuck in a loop of negotiation disputes. Much like how [Kenya Power maintenance outages](https://zandani.co.ke/article/kenya-power-scheduled-maintenance-outages-five-counties) cause immediate disruption to your home electricity, a failure in public sector salary processing causes a quiet but devastating blackout in service continuity.

### What are the risks of this ongoing impasse?

The standoff poses a direct threat to the stability of essential services. If the SRC enforces new reforms without union buy-in, the immediate result will likely be widespread industrial action. We have seen such patterns before, where hospitals are shuttered and administrative offices close their doors while workers take to the streets.

For the average citizen, this translates to a loss of time and money as they seek alternative private services for needs the government is no longer meeting. The situation is exacerbated by the broader economic context where public funds are already stretched thin. Both the government and the workers are essentially fighting over a shrinking pool of resources, and neither side appears willing to compromise on the structure of the deal. Unless a middle ground is found, the friction between the commission and the unions will continue to bottle up the funds that keep the public sector functional. Whether you are seeking a permit or attending to a medical emergency, the efficiency of your interactions with government offices depends on the resolution of this standoff.

A breakdown in salary negotiations is not just a policy issue, but a disruption to the daily life of every Kenyan.
