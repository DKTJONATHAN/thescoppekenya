## The Digital Gallows: Why Kenyans Love To Bury People Who Are Alive Former Tourism CS Najib Balala Dismisses Death Rumors is the central subject of the update.

"Someone needs to tell these bloggers that my life is not a commodity for their likes," is the sentiment echoing through digital corridors today. The internet is buzzing with frustration, not because of the death itself, but because of the callous greed behind the content that fabricated it. Kenyans are increasingly exhausted by a culture where a person's existence is treated as a gambling chip for engagement metrics.

Former Tourism Cabinet Secretary Najib Balala was forced to manually interrupt a Saturday morning of rumors to confirm he is not, in fact, deceased. The misinformation campaign started on peripheral social media pages before cascading into private WhatsApp groups, where a lack of fact checking turned a baseless claim into a national panic. Balala, who currently serves in a high profile capacity with the World Travel and Tourism Council, had to pivot from his professional obligations to reassure his family and colleagues that he is healthy and active. This incident marks yet another chapter in a long history of public figures being subjected to digital death sentences, a trend that dates back to the early days of Kenyan social media, with similar baseless hoaxes surfacing periodically in 2008, 2012, 2015, 2020, and 2022.

### The Ecosystem of Engagement Hunting

The speed at which this falsehood traveled highlights a systemic rot in the way information flows through Kenyan digital circles. Fringe content creators often target high profile figures to generate immediate curiosity, knowing that morbid subject matter triggers the algorithm faster than anything else. For these actors, the human impact-the shock felt by a distant relative or the professional disruption caused to a person's current duties-is merely collateral damage in a quest for clicks. It is a predatory business model that turns the sanctity of life into a transactional piece of content, mirroring the way some individuals approach aggressive [wealthy investment strategies](https://zandani.co.ke/article/wealthy-invest-differently-middle-class-investment-strategies) as a game rather than a calculated path to stability.

### Voices from the Digital Frontline

Discussions on X and various Facebook forums reveal a public that is increasingly cynical about the reliability of their own timelines. A Nairobi based media student noted that the speed of the rumour was only matched by the laziness of the source, pointing out that not a single shred of evidence was provided to back the claim. Another user observed that the obsession with being the first to report something often blinds creators to the reality that they are peddling a lie that deeply hurts real human beings. A veteran digital strategist suggested that these hoaxes act as a litmus test for the lack of digital literacy, arguing that as long as people continue to share without verifying, the digital gravediggers will continue to thrive.

### Where Does The Moral Compass Go?

The burden of proof now rests on both the consumer and the regulator. While high profile figures like Balala have the platform to strike back and expose these accounts, ordinary citizens rarely have the same reach when their reputations are damaged by similar rumors. The incident serves as a stark reminder that the digital space is still largely a lawless frontier. Moving forward, the focus must shift toward holding these content farms accountable for the trauma they inflict. Just as the aviation industry recently saw shifts in operational standards with [Çelebi Aviation taking over ground handling at JKIA](https://zandani.co.ke/article/celebi-aviation-turkish-airlines-ground-handling-jkia), there is an urgent need for a more rigid standard of conduct for those who manage information flow in the country.

The public mood remains one of profound irritation, signaling that while Kenyans are addicted to the speed of social media, they are growing increasingly resentful of the digital vultures feeding on the lives of others.
