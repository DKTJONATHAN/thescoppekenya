## The El Niño Mirage: Why 18 Counties Remain Trapped in a Cycle of Reactive Disaster Management

The Interior Ministry has once again flagged 18 counties as high-risk zones for the upcoming El Niño season, yet these same regions appeared on the exact same hazard list in 2025. Predictability in climate patterns is currently being met with a staggering institutional inability to break the cycle of disaster recurrence.

### The Fiscal Illusion of Preparedness

Each year, the government justifies massive emergency budgetary allocations under the banner of disaster preparedness. However, a forensic look at the audit trails reveals that these billions rarely translate into permanent, flood-resistant infrastructure. Instead, the funds are habitually funneled into reactive short-term measures like sandbag distributions and temporary food relief programs. In urban hubs such as Nairobi and Kisumu, the much-touted drainage masterplans remain perpetually stalled. Despite consistent funding cycles, the capital city continues to rely on colonial-era drainage networks that are choked by uncollected waste and structural neglect.

The disconnect between planning and execution is not an accident of climate uncertainty but a deliberate feature of local governance. When funds designated for drainage improvement are redirected into recurrent operational costs, the local government essentially bets that the rains will either bypass the region or that the resulting damage can be categorized as an unforeseen act of God. This accounting maneuver allows officials to avoid answering why drainage systems in high-risk zones are non-functional before the first cloud even gathers.

### Veterinary Neglect and Public Health Risks

Beyond the crumbling concrete, the threat of Rift Valley Fever looms as a direct consequence of long-term policy abandonment. The ICPAC warning regarding the potential for massive disease outbreaks across East Africa is not just an epidemiological forecast. It is a damning indictment of the systematic failure to enforce routine livestock vaccination schedules. In regions like Tana River, Garissa, and Wajir, the capacity to contain viral spread hinges on consistent, year-round veterinary outreach.

Instead, the livestock sector is treated as an emergency response project rather than a stable agricultural foundation. By failing to maintain vaccination cycles during dry spells, the state ensures that once the rains arrive and the mosquito vectors proliferate, the resulting epidemic will necessitate yet another emergency procurement of vaccines at inflated costs. This creates a lucrative loop for suppliers, while pastoralist communities are left to count the cost of lost herds. Even as news cycles focus on the spectacle of other national distractions, such as [James Orengo remembering the Raila Odinga partnership](https://zandani.co.ke/article/james-orengo-remembers-raila-odinga-partnership), the reality in these 18 counties remains one of deliberate administrative paralysis.

### The Pattern of Institutional Amnesia

The 2026 forecast confirms that the risks-ranging from landslides in West Pokot and Baringo to infrastructure collapse in the coastal counties of Mombasa, Kwale, and Kilifi-are precisely where they were twelve months ago. The recurring nature of this list suggests that there is no intent to solve the underlying problems. If the problems were solved, the emergency procurement budgets, which often lack the stringent oversight applied to capital projects, would disappear.

While the state prepares to broadcast images of officials distributing blankets and maize in the coming weeks, no amount of photo-opportunity politics will replace a functioning storm-drainage system or a proactive, year-round veterinary service. The country is watching a repeat performance of 2025, where the script of disaster is written well in advance, yet the actors pretend to be surprised by the arrival of the rain. At what point does an avoidable disaster cease to be a natural phenomenon and start to be classified as a standard administrative policy?
