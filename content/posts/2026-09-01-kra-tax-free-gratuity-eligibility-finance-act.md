## Will Your Final Gratuity Payout Face a Tax Penalty?

The Kenya Revenue Authority has released specific guidelines under the Finance Act 2026 that change how long-term employees collect their end-of-contract bonuses. These rules shift the tax-free status of your payout based strictly on your tenure and the total percentage of your earnings.

### How Does the Three-Year and 31 Per Cent Rule Work?

The KRA now applies a dual-filter test to determine if your gratuity is exempt from income tax. First, the employment period must be continuous and span at least three years. Second, the lump sum payment must not exceed 31 per cent of the total basic earnings accrued during that specific contract term.

If your contract ends even one day before the three-year mark, the entire payout becomes taxable as standard income. Similarly, if your gratuity exceeds the 31 per cent threshold, the excess amount is subject to the usual tax brackets. For example, if an employee finishes a 36-month contract with a gratuity calculated at 35 per cent of their total earnings, they will pay income tax on that 4 per cent difference. This creates a clear distinction between workers who fulfill the long-term commitment criteria and those who fall short, potentially costing the latter thousands of shillings in unexpected deductions.

### Is the 31 Per Cent Cap Realistic for Kenyan Employers?

Tax consultants suggest that this 31 per cent ceiling is a middle-ground approach that aligns closely with many existing collective bargaining agreements. Many private sector firms typically offer gratuity at rates ranging from 25 to 30 per cent of basic pay for fixed-term contracts. By setting the cap at 31 per cent, the government is effectively standardizing industry practice while closing loopholes that allowed for excessive tax-free bonuses in executive compensation packages.

However, employees should verify their documentation today to avoid discrepancies. Many workers assume their gratuity will be tax-free by default, failing to account for how bonuses or allowances might push their total payout percentage above the statutory limit. If your contract states a fixed gratuity rate, cross-reference that figure against your total accumulated basic pay for the three-year period to ensure you remain within the tax-exempt zone.

### A Practical Checklist for Your Employment Contract

To determine if your current arrangement qualifies for the tax-free status, conduct a quick audit of your employment file. First, verify the official start date and the designated end date of your contract. If you are close to the three-year threshold, communicate with your human resources department to confirm there will be no lapses in service that could reset your tenure clock.

Second, pull your latest pay slips or the payroll ledger. Calculate the total basic pay you have earned from the first day of your contract to the projected final date. Apply the 31 per cent limit to that sum. If your promised gratuity is higher than this calculated amount, prepare for tax deductions on the surplus. Ensure your contract terms are explicit to avoid finding yourself in a higher tax bracket during your final month of service.
