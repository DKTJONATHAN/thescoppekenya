## When the Cell Door Stays Locked Against a Court Order

The tension outside the Kamkunji Police Station is thick enough to cut, with lawyers and supporters questioning whether a judge’s signature still holds weight against the authority of the state. As the clock ticks past the four-hour mark since the Kiambu High Court ordered the release of Mukurwe-ini MP John Kaguchia on a Ksh100,000 cash bail, the growing delay has transformed a standard legal procedure into a high-stakes standoff between the judiciary and the executive.

The Kiambu High Court issued the release order today, August 3, following the MP's arrest in the wake of fiery remarks made during a political rally in Nyeri. While his legal team, including Ndegwa Njiru, successfully argued for his release, the reality on the ground at the Kamkunji facility remains stagnant. Supporters claim that despite the court’s clear directive, police officers on duty have effectively blocked access to the perimeter, citing ambiguous internal protocols. This friction mirrors the broader anxiety seen recently when the [DCI accused Linda Wananchi politicians of arming followers linked to a supporter's death](https://zandani.co.ke/article/dci-accuses-linda-wananchi-politicians-arming-followers-supporter-death), suggesting a hardening stance by law enforcement against those they perceive as political agitators.

### Voices from the Perimeter

For the residents of Mukurwe-ini who traveled to Nairobi to stand in solidarity with their representative, the mood is one of profound skepticism. A local trader from the constituency expressed that the arrest feels like an attempt to silence dissenting voices, arguing that if a Member of Parliament cannot secure his freedom after a court order, the average citizen stands no chance against the machinery of the state.

Constitutional lawyers watching the scene are sounding the alarm on the precedent being set. Many are taking to social media to warn that the deliberate frustration of court orders by police officers is a dangerous departure from the rule of law. They point out that the judiciary is the final arbiter of constitutional rights, and when police decide which court orders to honor, they are essentially usurping judicial power.

Political insiders, including allies like Rigathi Gachagua, are framing the situation as a litmus test for the independence of state institutions. They argue that the hesitation to process the bail is not a mere bureaucratic hiccup but a calculated tactic to keep the MP in custody for as long as possible. The atmosphere is tense, as observers compare this to other recent state actions, such as the [transition of senior school exams to digital platforms](https://zandani.co.ke/article/knec-to-transition-senior-school-exams-to-digital-platforms), noting that administrative shifts are moving faster than the protection of individual liberties.

### The 48-Hour Accountability Window

The legal team representing Kaguchia is now preparing for a potential contempt of court application. Under current procedure, the respondents have 14 days to file their formal responses to the court’s order. However, the immediate urgency is the next 48 hours. If the police continue to ignore the bail mandate, the lawyers intend to file an urgent motion seeking to hold the relevant officers in contempt.

This window of time is critical. If the MP is not processed and released by the end of this period, the legal battle will shift from the specific charges against him to a broader institutional conflict. The court has clearly set the terms of his release, and the failure to comply is rapidly shifting public sentiment, with many wondering if the police are operating under a separate set of rules that ignore the bench entirely.

The perimeter at Kamkunji remains crowded, with the public watching the main gate not just for the release of one man, but for a sign that the rule of law is still functioning. For now, the heavy presence of security personnel ensures that the standoff persists, leaving everyone in a state of uneasy observation as the night approaches.

A quiet dread is settling over the streets as the city waits to see if the law will be followed or if the heavy hand of the police will win the day.
